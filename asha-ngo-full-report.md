# ASHA + NGO support: full research report

**ASHA Saath - a feasible visit plan and accountable local assistance**

Research cut-off: **8 October 2026, India**. Prepared for the team's chosen Healthcare 03 problem at Codefiesta 5.0.

## Read this first

The recommended product combines the required ASHA/ANM visit optimizer with one narrowly scoped assistance workflow. A visit produces a consented support need; the relevant public-service pathway is checked; a participating provider with the right catchment, eligibility and current capacity accepts or declines; delivery and beneficiary/worker confirmation are recorded; unresolved cases retain an owner and next action.

The proposal remains subject to local validation. No real NGO partnership, government production integration, money raised, clinical benefit, travel improvement or winning probability is claimed. A funded coordinator and real local service offers are prerequisites for a meaningful pilot.

The report catalogues **24 additional schemes/programmes** and **30 platforms/tools**. ASHA and the 2026 SSBSK operational framework are discussed separately, outside the additional 24 count. Related programmes, integrated systems, campaigns and overseas comparators are labelled; their beneficiaries and markets must not be added together. Shaishav is an officially described 2026 tool whose universal deployment was not verified.

Ten distinct research-agent assignments were used in batches: ASHA ground truth; maternal/child services; digital-health products; welfare/inclusion; NGO networks; public-service/livelihood programmes; civic access and financing; service handoffs and stakeholder perspectives; MVP engineering; and independent audit. The parent agent performed additional comparator research, synthesis and artifact preparation.

Evidence is distinguished as official documentation, provider-described capability, setting-specific research, design proposal or validation hypothesis. A feature missing from a public page is not automatically absent from the product. See the method note and the audit.

Evidence categories used throughout the report:

- **Official:** government guidelines, scheme pages, parliamentary answers and official review reports. Eligibility, rollout and benefits are date-sensitive.
- **Provider-documented:** product operators' descriptions and documentation. Features and claimed reach are not independently validated outcomes.
- **Study-specific:** published research with a particular geography, sample and period. Findings are not automatically national estimates.

Geographic coverage, staff capacity, funding availability, API access and willingness to participate must be confirmed with each partner. # Part 1. ASHA and the official problem statement

## ASHA/ANM ground truth for Healthcare 03 and an NGO-support extension

Research cutoff: **8 October 2026, India**. Prepared as one research contribution to the requested ten-agent review. All recommendations below are design proposals, not authorization from NHM and not clinical advice. Sources were checked with connected Tavily initially and the built-in web tool after Tavily research returned HTTP 504 and search reached its usage limit. No outreach, accounts, deployment, or movement of funds occurred.

### 1. What the hackathon actually asks for

The official problem-statement listing was successfully extracted on 8 October 2026 and confirms **Healthcare 03: Community Health Worker (ASHA/ANM) Visit Scheduling Optimizer with Risk-Weighted Prioritization**. The listing says 14 themes and 70 statements, released 8 October. Current extraction exposed the title, not the expanded panel. [Official Codefiesta 5.0 problem statements](https://hackathon.gitjaipur.com/ps).

The parent research brief records previously read expanded requirements: import a caseload CSV containing patient ID, location and rule-based risk; cluster visits and produce routes; prioritize with risk and travel estimates; display daily routes, risk flags and coverage. Those requirements are **carried forward from the earlier reading**, not claimed as text independently re-extracted here. The official panel should be saved or reread before presenting an exact quotation to judges.

The upgrade should therefore retain a visible working optimizer. A good demonstration starts with a CSV, explains why each household is due, produces a feasible daily visit sequence, and shows who could not be covered. NGO assistance is an additional response to barriers discovered during those visits. A general donation marketplace would weaken alignment with this PS.

### 2. The worker is a trusted community interface with a defined scope

NHM describes ASHA as a village-selected, trained woman linking communities to public health services. Duties include counselling, health education, mobilization for government services, limited protocol-appropriate first-contact care, and timely referrals. Selection generally prefers resident women aged 25–45 and gives preference to education through class 10, with relaxation when suitable candidates are unavailable. She receives performance incentives and depends on institutional support, including ANMs and Anganwadi workers. The NHM page has a website footer dated 14 September 2026; that footer is not proof that every item, including its old drug list, was freshly revised in 2026. [NHM: About ASHA](https://www.nhm.gov.in/images/index1.php?lang=1&level=1&lid=226&sublinkid=150).

NHSRC, NHM's technical support institute, frames ASHA's three functions as facilitating healthcare, providing defined community services, and health activism. Its current catalogue recognizes ASHAs within Ayushman Arogya Mandir primary-care teams and describes expanded community work including chronic disease, mental health, elderly and palliative care, alongside maternal/child and communicable-disease work. It lists continuing training, certification and support materials, including an April 2026 certification letter. These are trained tasks, not unrestricted medical authority. [NHSRC: Community Processes—ASHA](https://nhsrcindia.org/practice-areas/cpc-phc/community-process-asha).

#### Scope-to-product mapping (design recommendation)

| Person or organization | Appropriate product capability | Boundary to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| ASHA | Confirm household location and consent, view assigned visits, record observations using approved forms, identify a need, initiate escalation/referral, track follow-up | Do not ask her to diagnose unfamiliar illness, override a clinician, or become a fundraising case manager by default |
| ANM | Clinical assessment and service delivery within qualifications/protocols; validate relevant findings; oversee and join indicated visits | Do not equate an ASHA visit with an ANM examination, immunization, or clinical clearance |
| CHO/medical officer | Own locally approved clinical pathways and technical escalation; review complex cases and appropriate referral destination | Software rankings and NGO volunteers cannot substitute for this authority |
| ASHA facilitator/community-process supervisor | Help with workload, mentoring, missed households, logistics, training and quality | Keep supportive review distinct from diagnosis and from punitive GPS surveillance |
| AWW/Anganwadi centre | Nutrition and child-development linkage, eligible supplementary nutrition and coordination | Avoid inventing dietary treatment or treating charitable food as the child's entire care pathway |
| NGO caseworker/volunteer | Accept a specifically consented support request, confirm service capacity, arrange nonclinical assistance and report fulfillment | No automatic access to full patient records; no ability to change clinical risk classification or certify health outcomes |
| District/block program authority | Approve worker mappings, rules, partner participation and monitoring arrangements | A registration badge or app account is not government endorsement |

The official rural coordination table assigns ASHA mobilization/home education, ANM clinical outreach and joint support, and AWW nutrition responsibilities. The same guidelines place immediate field support with ANM and ASHA facilitator. They provide a rural norm of one ASHA per 1,000 people, allowing habitation-based relaxation for difficult terrain. They envisage approximately 3–4 hours of overall ASHA work daily on 4–5 days a week, with roughly two hours for home visits; these are historical planning norms, not observed 2026 hours. They also encourage competency-based NGO partnerships for training and support. [Community Processes Guidelines, catalogued as 2014; forewords dated 26 June 2013; pp. 5–13](https://nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2021-07/Community%20Processes%20Guidelines%202014%20%28English%29.pdf).

### 3. Geography and coverage are not interchangeable

Urban guidelines use one ASHA per **1,000–2,500 population**, approximately 200–500 households, with smaller coverage possible for dispersed or distinct vulnerable groups. Mahila Arogya Samitis generally cover 50–100 households, or 250–500 people. The framework targets vulnerable urban populations and gives states contextual flexibility. This is an older framework still hosted by NHM; the web search's recent crawl/publication labels should not be mistaken for its original issue date. [NUHM implementation framework, 2013-era framework](https://www.nhm.gov.in/images/pdf/NUHM/Implementation_Framework_NUHM.pdf); [NHM orientation module: selection norms](https://www.nhm.gov.in/images/pdf/NUHM/Orientation_module_for_planners_implementers_and_partners.pdf).

For scale, Parliament reported **1,030,992 ASHAs in position**, including **53,914 in Rajasthan**, using NHM-MIS data as of **30 September 2024**. The answer was issued **25 March 2025**. Those are dated counts, not an October 2026 census. It also explains that NHM releases state funds through flexible pools, rather than a separate ASHA allocation for every state. [Rajya Sabha question 2830, annexure I](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/267/AU2830_j5ckFi.pdf?source=pqars).

Design implications:

- Model the **assigned households**, not a blanket radius around a worker. Someone living outside the efficient cluster may still be her responsibility.
- Keep village/hamlet/ward, block, district, state, rural/urban and coordinates as different fields. Administrative matching and travel matching serve different purposes.
- Rural walking paths, seasonal accessibility and urban settlement entrances need manual correction. A map coordinate does not prove a road is usable.
- National NGO visibility is not local service availability. Match requests to verified district/ward coverage, current capacity, eligibility, opening times and actual contact channels.
- Repeatedly deferred, inaccessible, migrant or unlocated households belong in the coverage gap list. Do not quietly drop them to improve a distance metric.

### 4. Critical 2026 update: SSBSK, not a frozen HBNC/HBYC model

On **29 June 2026**, MoHFW launched **Samagra Shishu Bal Swasthya Karyakram (SSBSK)**, integrating HBNC and HBYC into home/community care from birth through **36 months**. Its announced approach includes coordinated ASHA, ANM, CHO and AWW visits and early childhood development. [PIB/MoHFW launch release, 29 June 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2279075&lang=1&reg=3).

The **25 August 2026** government workshop release confirms risk-differentiated care, additional follow-up for at-risk children, joint visits and clinical oversight. It describes **Shaishav App** for at-risk line-listing, scheduling/tracking visits, referral follow-up, supportive supervision and longitudinal child profiles. State-specific implementation readiness was under discussion. This verifies the policy direction and an existing digital product; it does **not** establish uniform adoption in every district by October. [PIB/MoHFW SSBSK workshop, 24–25 August 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2303141&lang=2&reg=48).

NHM lists **SSBSK Operational Guidelines 2026**, alongside legacy HBNC and HBYC documents. The official listing was read, but the 67.1 MB guideline PDF could not be retrieved or its exact visit table independently verified in this run. An unofficial mirror contains purported new schedules; it is deliberately excluded from clinical defaults here. **Do not label legacy schedules below as the unchanged current SSBSK schedule.** Obtain the official 2026 table and the pilot district's approved implementation instructions before configuring production rules. [NHM SSBSK guideline catalogue, website updated 7 October 2026](https://nhm.gov.in/index4.php?lang=1&level=0&lid=825&linkid=521).

#### Verified legacy schedules for a clearly labelled demonstration

NHM's community-programme page still gives HBNC visits after institutional birth on days **3, 7, 14, 21, 28 and 42**, with an additional visit within 24 hours for home delivery. It identifies additional follow-up for preterm, low-birth-weight, ill and SNCU-discharged babies. Its HBYC description gives visits at **3, 6, 9, 12 and 15 months**. Both descriptions are legacy programmes; this page's August 2026 website timestamp does not resolve the transition to SSBSK. [NHM community-based child health programme](https://nhm.gov.in/index4.php?lang=1&level=0&lid=818&linkid=514).

An older official urban ASHA training module corroborates those HBNC dates and teaches prompt facility referral for danger signs. It places pregnancy assessment/referral decisions with trained facility personnel and ANMs. This provides useful role separation but should not be mined for current medication doses or modern diagnostic thresholds. [NHSRC urban ASHA induction module, publication day not stated in retrieved text; hosted under 2021 directory](https://nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2021-06/Induction%20Training%20Module%20for%20ASHAs%20in%20Urban%20Areas%20English.pdf).

Recommended rule record: `rule_id`, `program`, `version`, `source_url`, `published_date`, `effective_date_if_known`, `district_approval`, `eligible_cohort`, `due_date/window`, `required_cadre`, `joint_visit_required`, `escalation_path`, `review_date`. Unknown or conflicting rules should be shown to the supervisor; no silent rule switching.

### 5. Incentives and workload: avoid false current-pay claims

The official answer to **Rajya Sabha question 276, 22 July 2025** states that the fixed monthly incentive for routine/recurring activities was revised from ₹2,000 to **₹3,500** at the **4 March 2025** Mission Steering Group meeting. Task/performance incentives and state top-ups also apply; ASHAs remain envisaged as community health volunteers. This is the latest dated central revision directly verified here. It does not mean ₹3,500 is salary, total monthly earnings, guaranteed receipt, or that there were no later state changes. [Official Parliament answer](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/268/AU276_yO0C1A.pdf?source=pqars).

A separate **July 2025 Lok Sabha answer** describes state-funded top-ups, AAM team incentives up to ₹1,000 monthly, nonmonetary support such as cycles and phones, and a ₹50,000 recognition amount for eligible leavers with at least ten years of service. Keep these dated and eligibility-specific. The exact parliamentary day was not recovered in this run. The NHM July 2025 incentive-table URL cited by Parliament returned a 404 during direct retrieval, so task-by-task amounts were not treated as newly verified. [Lok Sabha question 1082, 2025](https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/185/AU1082_yJV2q5.pdf?source=pqals).

Observed workload differs from a guideline allowance. A primary mixed-method study published in **2025** covered **26 ASHAs** in one urban and one rural medical-college field-practice area in Bengaluru, with fieldwork in **June–July 2024**. Ten participants reported 5–6 hours daily; some described delayed or incomplete incentives. This small local study supports the plausibility of workload and payment friction, not a national 2026 average. [Primary study: work sampling assessment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12296319/).

NHSRC's **FY 2024–25 work report**, hosted in February 2026, records a dedicated ASHA workload assessment and says its final report had been shared for presentation to MoHFW. The retrieved passage does not provide its numerical findings. Therefore no national hours-worked statistic is asserted from it. [NHSRC work report FY 2024–25, p. 38](https://nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/NHSRC%20Work%20report%20FY%202024-25%20Eng.pdf).

Product implications:

- Worker-set availability, caregiving responsibilities, travel time, service time, existing outreach sessions, review/training meetings and escort duties must fit the day before adding NGO tasks.
- Show planned minutes, buffer, overruns and reasons; allow the ASHA to correct estimates.
- Measure time saved and fewer missed essential visits. Do not assume more visits per day always improves care.
- Separate clinically urgent priority from financial incentives. A scoring model should not favor whichever task pays more.
- Support requests should have an NGO/district coordinator owner. ASHA should not need to chase every donor or upload repeated proofs to complete her own visits.
- Record support completion independently of clinical visit completion and legitimate incentive documentation.

MoHFW's **Annual Report 2024–25** describes RCH as individual reproductive/child lifecycle tracking, and ANMOL as an ANM application with service due lists, workplans, VHND sessions, a dashboard, digital RCH register and counselling aids. It reported ANMOL in 28 states/UTs and approximately 1.82 lakh registered ANMs at that older report's reference point. Its list omits Rajasthan; do not assume the pilot uses ANMOL just because it is a national app. [MoHFW Annual Report 2024–25, section 1.2.6](https://www.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/Final%20Printed%20English%20AR%202024-25.pdf). **Newer chronology:** the 2025–26 report states 29 States/UTs and about 1.82 lakh ANMs as of 31 December 2025; use that dated figure for the current platform comparison. [MoHFW Annual Report 2025–26, p.50](https://notto.mohfw.gov.in/WriteReadData/Portal/News/951_1_Annual_Report_of_the_MoH_FW__2025_-_2026.pdf).

On **24 March 2026**, MoHFW reported that U-WIN already line-lists children due for vaccination, sends reminders and generates lists of people who did not attend for follow-up. Counts were explicitly as of **18 March 2026**. A scheduling app should not advertise vaccination due-list generation as a first-of-its-kind invention. [PIB: Update on U-WIN, 24 March 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2244518&lang=1&reg=1).

It can demonstrate import/export using a synthetic CSV. ### 7. NGO support already has a policy foothold, but must fit the health system

Urban ASHA/MAS guidelines explicitly describe NGOs as additional capacity for training and supportive community processes. They require rigorous, transparent selection, defined terms of reference and integration with the urban health system. This is a stronger basis than inventing a parallel clinical chain. [NUHM guidelines for ASHA and MAS, NGO section p. 23](https://www.nhm.gov.in/images/pdf/NUHM/Guidelines_for_Asha_and_MAS_in_Urban_Context.pdf).

Government entitlements should be checked before a charitable funding request. Under **JSSK**, NHM states that eligible public-facility delivery and specified pregnancy/infant care include free drugs, diagnostics, blood, diet and transport, including referral transport. The scheme launched **1 June 2011** and extended relevant entitlements in **2014**; the retrieved page was website-updated July 2026. Availability failures should be escalated and tracked, rather than automatically treated as a donor's obligation. [NHM: Janani-Shishu Suraksha Karyakram](https://nhm.gov.in/nhm_components/Empanelment_of_Independent_National_Monitors/index1.php?lang=1&level=3&lid=308&sublinkid=842).

#### Recommended referral workflow (design proposal)

1. **Plan the health visit.** Pull due tasks from authorized records or the demo CSV. Preserve scheduled visits and required cadre.
2. **Escalate urgent findings immediately through the locally approved clinical route.** Routine optimization must not defer an emergency to tomorrow or await NGO acceptance.
3. **Record a distinct support barrier.** Examples: cannot reach the appointment, missing documents for entitlement, unavailable caregiver, nutrition access, disability access or unsafe travel.
4. **Check government entitlement and existing facility/Anganwadi pathways.** Ask what assistance is actually missing.
5. **Obtain specific consent for a limited NGO referral.** Select necessary fields and recipient. Consent to a health visit is not consent to public fundraising.
6. **Match a partner by actual service.** Confirm geographic coverage, eligibility, cost, capacity, access hours, quality/safeguarding requirements and the date of the latest operational confirmation.
7. **Have the partner accept or decline.** A directory match is not a promise. Provide a coordinator fallback and expiry/escalation for an unanswered request.
8. **Coordinate fulfillment with the scheduled health care.** The NGO may arrange a ride or accompaniment; ANM/CHO/facility retains the clinical decision.
9. **Confirm completion with minimal evidence.** Record who accepted, when support happened and who followed up; protect household identity.
10. **Close two loops separately.** Support supplied does not mean referral completed or clinical risk resolved. A health worker confirms the latter within her role.

#### Candidate support types, without claims about any particular NGO

| Support need | Appropriate potential NGO contribution | What the app must verify |
|---|---|---|
| Travel barrier | Accessible transport or accompaniment; transport vouchers administered by an accountable partner | Local availability, cost, consent, safeguarding and whether public transport entitlement already applies |
| Nutrition access | Entitlement help, delivery/access coordination, suitable food assistance under approved counselling | Program eligibility, actual supply, approved plan and follow-up owner |
| Documents/benefits | Help completing forms and accessing schemes | No sale of data, no retention of unnecessary Aadhaar/bank information, no promise of approval |
| Disability/elder support | Accessible equipment referral, mobility aid, caregiver support | Qualified assessment where required, correct fit, maintenance and service capacity |
| Health literacy | Approved-language education, local mobilization and digital assistance | Training, language, supervision and no unsupported clinical advice |
| Supplies | Funds or approved procurement for equipment, connectivity or logistics through an accountable organization | Procurement policy, stock, quality, responsible custodian and duplication with government supply |
| Treatment-related financial barrier | Case navigation and assistance through a verified organization's own accountable process | Eligibility, legitimacy, documented expenses, benefit coverage and protection of patient identity |

Funding should be a later, explicitly governed support feature. In the hackathon MVP, show **noncash request, partner acceptance, fulfillment and audit history**. If a funding scenario is displayed, use synthetic data and a labelled simulated ledger. Do not pool money, promise a medical outcome, rank households by donor appeal, charge workers referral fees, or let an NGO payment alter clinical priority. A verified organization's existing funding process is a safer next-step concept than making this prototype a payment intermediary.

### 8. Preserve the optimizer and add accountable support

Recommended ordering for the scheduling engine:

1. Immediate locally defined escalation bypasses ordinary route scoring.
2. Required program dates/windows, indicated post-discharge follow-up and joint-cadre availability constrain the plan.
3. Risk tier, overdue duration and repeated missed coverage influence priority using explainable, approved rules.
4. Availability, care duration, route/access constraints and capacity determine feasibility.
5. Distance/time minimization chooses among clinically acceptable alternatives.

Show a reason for each priority, the rule/source/version, travel estimate confidence and a supervisor override with a reason. If mandatory work exceeds capacity, report the conflict and request coordination rather than fabricate a feasible schedule. A distant household's priority should not decay simply because it costs more time to reach.

Recommended MVP outputs: daily worker route, risk and due-date explanation, required joint visits, estimated travel/care time, unvisited/overdue queue, map correction, referral log, limited partner request and partner acceptance/decline. Suggested evidence metrics: risk-weighted visits completed before deadlines; essential overdue visits; travel minutes per valid visit; hard-to-reach household coverage; successful clinical referral follow-up; time to accepted nonclinical assistance; ASHA time spent on data entry/support chasing. All clinical outcome claims require a real evaluation beyond a hackathon.

### 9. Verification gaps to resolve before a pilot

- Exact SSBSK 2026 schedule/risk criteria from the **official PDF**, and state/district adoption and training status.
- The pilot site's existing system: RCH/ANMOL/Shaishav/Janani/U-WIN or a state implementation; permissible data exchange and duplication burden.
- Locally approved escalation destination, coverage hours, ambulance/transport arrangements and service capacity.
- Who actually approves the rule pack and NGO participation, and how the ASHA will access help when offline.
- Each partner's real district/ward service availability, eligibility, capacity, request channel and safeguarding practice. Registration alone does not verify delivery capability.
- Current state-specific incentive orders and actual local payment workflows if incentive tracking is included.
- Real observed travel/care times, missed-visit reasons and worker-set availability. Older national norms and small studies should not be substituted for local observation.

The proposed upgrade is credible when a high-priority visit leads to an accepted, consented, traceable support action that helps the person complete authorized care. Its strength is connecting operational barriers to responsible local assistance while retaining the original risk-weighted route planner.

The table is a comparison of pathways, **not eight additive populations or eight NGO funding sources**. JSY, PMMVY, JSSK, PMSMA and SUMAN can concern the same pregnancy. MI delivers UIP vaccines; beneficiaries and doses must not be added twice. PM-JAY is a hospital benefit, distinct from primary-care services and from an ABHA identifier.

### 1. Janani Suraksha Yojana — JSY

**Purpose and eligibility.** JSY promotes institutional delivery to reduce maternal/neonatal mortality. NHM's current page distinguishes low performing states (LPS) from high performing states (HPS): all women delivering in government facilities in LPS; BPL/SC/ST women in HPS government facilities; BPL/SC/ST women in accredited private facilities in either group. The same page lists mother's cash packages as LPS ₹1,400 rural/₹1,000 urban and HPS ₹700 rural/₹600 urban. ASHA packages are **₹600 rural and ₹400 urban**, separate from the mother's benefit; the national page splits them into ANC and delivery-facilitation components. Do not advertise a combined mother-plus-ASHA figure as the mother's payment. Check the state's current classification and instructions, particularly where the national page retains legacy geography. [NHM JSY page](https://nhm.gov.in/nhm/index1.php?lang=1&level=3&lid=309&sublinkid=841) (publication date unstated; site footer 7 Oct 2026; accessed 8 Oct 2026).

**Where/how to refer.** National centrally sponsored scheme under NHM, implemented by states/districts. Identify the woman's ASHA/ANM and a delivery-capable government subcentre, PHC, CHC, first-referral unit or district/state hospital, or a currently accredited private institution. Obtain the facility's own eligibility/document checklist, record the submission acknowledgment, and separately track whether the mother's payment was received. Facility accreditation is a referral check, not an assumption based on private ownership.

**ASHA and NGO complement.** ASHA links the woman to ANC, delivery and post-delivery care. An NGO can help with language access, document preparation, travel planning, appointment reminders and payment-status escalation, with the woman's consent. An NGO's support should strengthen the ASHA/ANM/facility workflow, not make eligibility decisions or collect the woman's benefit.

**Bottleneck evidence.** CAG's Haryana Report No. 2 of 2024 documented delayed/non-payment and missing records in tested facilities for earlier periods, including 2016–21. This establishes a historical, location-specific payment-tracking problem; it does **not** establish the October 2026 delay rate in a new district. Missing banking/documents or late submission are operational risks to test locally, not claimed national prevalence. [CAG Haryana public-health audit](https://cag.gov.in/webroot/uploads/download_audit_report/2024/English_Final_Haryana-Report-No2-06734a09867e211.27299110.pdf) (2024, chapter 7).

**NGO funding status.** JSY payments are beneficiary/ASHA entitlements under applicable rules. No automatic NGO grant or outreach contract was verified. Discuss a costed support proposal with the District Health Society/state NHM only through a documented procurement or partnership route.

### 2. Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram — JSSK

**Purpose and benefit.** JSSK addresses out-of-pocket costs of public-facility delivery and sick-infant treatment. The NHM page confirms free delivery including C-section, drugs/consumables, diagnostics, blood when required, maternal diet for three days after normal delivery and seven after C-section, and home-to-facility/interfacility/drop-back transport. Entitlements extend to antenatal/postnatal pregnancy complications and sick newborns/infants **up to one year** in public institutions. These are service entitlements, not a cash transfer or a promise of equivalent reimbursement at any private hospital. [NHM JSSK page](https://nhm.gov.in/nhm_live/index1.php?lang=1&level=3&lid=308&sublinkid=842) (launch June 2011; extension recorded 2014; site footer 6 Oct 2026; accessed 8 Oct 2026).

**Coverage and referral.** National public-health initiative, operationalized by states through rural and urban public facilities and transport/referral systems. Map the nearest delivery point, C-section/referral hospital and facility able to treat a sick infant. Ask the ANM or facility to confirm transport booking, referral contact, diagnostics and required supplies; ASHA can explain entitlements and accompany/facilitate an appropriate referral within local duties. A family should not wait for a benefit application before seeking urgent clinical care.

**NGO complement.** Provide a local-language entitlement card, help families contact the public transport/facility team, support accessible navigation, and document charges or denial for beneficiary-led escalation. If an NGO offers backup travel or accompaniment from its own funds, clearly label it as NGO support and record the reason; it is not evidence that JSSK's public transport entitlement has disappeared.

**Bottleneck evidence.** The NHM page records high delivery-related expenses as the scheme's original rationale. Its historical background percentages must not be presented as current prevalence. Transport non-arrival, supply gaps and inappropriate charging are **local operational risks** requiring district service records or a current audit before being described as demonstrated failures.

**Funding.** No automatic NGO grant was verified. Referral-transport or other service contracts, where invited by a state, require their own tender, authorization and service standards; do not infer a contract merely because JSSK provides transport.

### 3. Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana — PMMVY

**Purpose, amounts and milestones.** PMMVY partially compensates maternity wage loss and promotes health-seeking. WCD's official FAQ specifies first-child ₹5,000 as ₹3,000 after pregnancy registration and at least two ANC checks, preferably within six months of LMP, plus ₹2,000 after registered birth and completion of the first immunization cycle at fourteen weeks. For a **second child who is a girl**, ₹6,000 is paid in one instalment after the corresponding pregnancy, birth and immunization conditions. JSY can also apply, subject to its own rules; do not promise a fixed combined total. Citizen application and ASHA/AWW-assisted online application are supported. [WCD PMMVY page](https://www.wcd.gov.in/women/pradhan-mantri-matru-vandana-yojna) (date unstated; accessed 8 Oct 2026).

**Eligibility caveats.** Accepted categories include SC/ST, disability of at least 40%, BPL/NFSA ration card, PM-JAY, e-Shram, MGNREGA job card, PM-Kisan beneficiary, family income below ₹8 lakh per year, and pregnant/lactating AWW/AWH/ASHA, with prescribed proof. This is not universal unconditional payment to every pregnancy. The 18 July 2023 order adds NFSA ration-card eligibility and clarifies income-proof acceptance. The 17 April 2023 order specifies age **18 years seven months–55**; the FAQ describes eligibility at childbirth as under 55. The 20 February 2023 order sets an application window of **270 days after childbirth**. Check portal application of the endpoint and any subsequent state instruction. [Eligibility order](https://wcd.gov.in/images/uploaded/1710112241_amP5P6rxQB.pdf), [age order](https://wcd.gov.in/images/uploaded/1710112241_5DWkILT5au.pdf), [time-limit order](https://wcd.gov.in/images/uploaded/1710112241_fm5Lgec9R2.pdf).

Mission Shakti guidelines exclude regular Central/State Government or PSU employees and women receiving similar maternity benefits under another law. For second-girl-child benefits, registration **during pregnancy is mandatory**; do not interpret the later application window as permission to omit pregnancy registration. The current FAQ allows fresh-beneficiary treatment after miscarriage/stillbirth in a future pregnancy and removes mandatory husband's Aadhaar. [Mission Shakti implementation guidelines](https://wcd.gov.in/documents/uploaded/1714563459_tx4qgk0riq.pdf) (2022 framework; accessed 8 Oct 2026).

**Where and current geography.** Use the nearest AWC/ASHA, state-approved health facility and [PMMVY citizen portal](https://pmmvy.wcd.gov.in). A **12 August 2026** parliamentary reply says implementation is in **35 States/UTs including Odisha**, which began FY 2025–26; **Telangana was still being onboarded**. This newer source supersedes the FAQ's older statement excluding both Odisha and Telangana. Confirm current Telangana onboarding before routing applications. [PIB/WCD update](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2298273&reg=3&lang=1).

**ASHA/NGO workflow.** With consent, check one accepted eligibility proof, beneficiary Aadhaar, Aadhaar-mapped bank/post-office account, mobile number, MCP/RCH records, ANC, birth registration and vaccine records. ASHA/AWW can submit and track within authorized jurisdiction. An NGO can assist the woman to obtain corrections from the actual bank/civil-registration/health authority, preserve acknowledgment and refer unresolved cases to the local administering officer. [PMMVY 2.0 FAQ](https://wcd.gov.in/images/uploaded/1710098119_HDEXdpWMb1.pdf) (undated document; accessed 8 Oct 2026).

**Bottleneck and funding.** The official FAQ evidences documentary/payment prerequisites; whether Aadhaar-bank mismatch, missing birth records or connectivity causes delays locally is a **hypothesis to audit**, not a verified nationwide rate. The ministry announced **1515** for POSHAN/PMMVY from 1 Nov 2025; confirm local connection. Benefit money is not NGO income; ASHA/AWW honorarium rules do not create NGO entitlement. [Helpline announcement, 24 Oct 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2182173&reg=48&lang=2).

### 4. Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan — PMSMA

**Purpose and eligibility.** PMSMA offers free quality ANC for pregnant women in the **second or third trimester** at designated government facilities on the **ninth of each month**. The official portal describes diagnostics/counselling, detection and line-listing of high-risk pregnancy, management of conditions and birth/complication planning, with specialist/physician review. Confirm the locally scheduled session; an emergency must use the immediate clinical referral route. [Official PMSMA portal](https://pmsma.mohfw.gov.in/?lang=en) (program introduced 2016; portal carries September 2026 activity; accessed 8 Oct 2026).

**Geography and pathway.** National program under MoHFW; states and districts identify participating rural/urban government sites and referral facilities. An NGO should obtain the local facility roster, session timetable and named high-risk referral contact from the district maternal-health team. Routine ANC remains necessary between program visits.

**ASHA and NGO complement.** Official guidelines assign ASHA awareness, beneficiary line-listing and mobilization; ANM/facility staff provide ANC and organize clinical referral. NGOs can support appointment reminders, interpretation, transport/navigation and tracking whether the referred woman reached the treating facility. Qualified obstetricians/radiologists/physicians may explore the official volunteer registration channel; NGO affiliation alone does not authorize clinical practice. [PMSMA operational guidelines](https://pmsma.mohfw.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Pradhan_Mantri_Surakshit_Matritva_Abhiyan.pdf) (2016 document; sections on ASHA/ANM roles).

**Bottleneck evidence.** Missed ANC and high-risk pregnancy identification are explicit program objectives. A specialist being absent, diagnostics being unavailable or a high-risk referral failing to close are **site-specific risks** to verify from attendance, test and referral records; the reviewed sources do not prove their current district prevalence.

**Funding.** The volunteering channel is a service opportunity, not an NGO grant or salary commitment. Any funded outreach, transport or clinical arrangement requires its own state/district authorization and terms.

### 5. Surakshit Matritva Aashwasan — SUMAN

**Purpose and entitlement boundary.** SUMAN aims for dignified, respectful and quality maternal/newborn care at no cost, with zero tolerance for denial in public facilities. Its service charter includes at least four ANC checks, home-based newborn-care visits, trained delivery, complication management, breastfeeding support, transport/referral, newborn vaccination and grievance redressal. It integrates existing initiatives including JSSK and PMSMA. It is **not a separately additive cash benefit**; the charter refers beneficiaries to conditional transfers under the applicable schemes. [Official SUMAN portal and charter](https://www.suman.mohfw.gov.in/) (page last updated 10 July 2021; accessed through official indexed content 8 Oct 2026).

**Geography and pathway.** National assurance initiative implemented through states' public maternity/newborn networks. Ask the district for its current designated SUMAN facilities and referral map, and confirm which level handles normal delivery, obstetric complications and sick newborn care. The older portal's reference to referral within an hour is a service-guarantee aspiration; do not promise an actual travel time for every village.

**ASHA and NGO complement.** ASHA can explain entitlements, help birth planning and link facility care to authorized home follow-up. NGOs can provide language/accessibility support, birth-companion navigation where facility policy permits, beneficiary feedback and grievance assistance. Record the woman's account of a service denial with consent, retain referral/receipt details, and help her approach the facility help desk and district maternal-health officer. The portal lists a grievance number **1800-180-1104** and web registration; because its page is dated 2021, validate the working channel before printing it locally.

**Bottleneck evidence.** A zero-denial charter and complaint mechanism establish standards; they do not establish how frequently denial occurs in 2026. Poor respectful-care experiences, missed home visits and inaccessible complaints are **implementation hypotheses** until supported by a current facility audit or beneficiary feedback sample.

**Funding.** No automatic NGO grant was verified. Community engagement can be proposed to state/district NHM through a written partnership, approved outreach plan or procurement; any NGO clinical service requires distinct permissions.

### 6. Mission Indradhanush — MI, including intensified rounds

**Purpose and beneficiaries.** MI targets missed and partially vaccinated children and pregnant women, particularly underserved/high-risk settlements, through intensified routine-immunization outreach. **IMI 5.0 was a dated 2023 campaign**, conducted in all districts, including children up to five years and pregnant women; previous rounds commonly used younger child age bands. It delivered vaccines already in UIP according to the National Immunization Schedule. Do not describe IMI 5.0's August–October 2023 sessions as an open October 2026 campaign. [MoHFW/PIB IMI 5.0 release, 12 Oct 2023](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1966931&lang=2&reg=3).

**Geography and access.** National strategy implemented through state/district microplans and notified rounds; rural hamlets, remote habitations, urban slums and migratory/temporary settlements are practical outreach targets. Ask the District Immunization Officer/ANM whether a current catch-up round is notified and where sessions will operate. If no campaign session is current, route due vaccines to the continuing UIP service.

**ASHA and NGO complement.** Under ANM/district microplanning, ASHA/AWW/community mobilizers identify due beneficiaries and bring families to sessions. NGO staff can help find missed mobile families, translate approved information, address travel/time barriers and support reminders, while trained authorized vaccinators administer and document vaccines. Vaccine hesitancy or access barriers should be discussed respectfully; do not claim an NGO can create independent government immunization records or access U-WIN through its own system without approval.

**Bottleneck evidence.** The official 2023 release identifies missed/dropped-out beneficiaries as the campaign's target. Local reasons—migration, session timing, misinformation, transport or stock availability—must be measured rather than assumed.

**Overlap and accounting.** MI is a campaign mechanism **within the UIP immunization ecosystem**, not a second vaccine entitlement. Use one child/pregnancy record and one dose event. Tag the event's delivery setting as MI/catch-up if relevant; do not sum MI participants with UIP participants to estimate unique reach, and do not count each return dose as a new beneficiary.

**Funding.** No automatic NGO grant was verified. Support requires a district-approved campaign role, a formal implementation arrangement where available, or the NGO's own funding.

### 7. Universal Immunization Programme — UIP

**Purpose and benefit.** UIP is the continuing national routine program providing free scheduled vaccination for children/adolescents and pregnant women. MoHFW's 2024–25 annual report supplies an updated national schedule including newborn and infant doses, later boosters, Td at ages ten/sixteen and pregnancy Td, with Japanese encephalitis vaccination in endemic districts. Clinical staff must determine due/catch-up doses from the current schedule and past records. Do not use an old web PDF showing phased vaccine introduction as proof of October 2026 availability. [MoHFW Annual Report 2024–25, immunization chapter and schedule, p. 72](https://mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/Final%20Printed%20English%20AR%202024-25.pdf).

**Geography and service point.** National program, delivered by state/district immunization teams through fixed public-facility and outreach sessions in both rural and urban areas. Local access includes village/AWC/subcentre sessions, PHCs/UPHCs and hospitals. Maharashtra's state NHM page explicitly describes free institution/outreach vaccination and illustrates state-specific endemic-district arrangements; its details must not be copied as another state's service map. [Maharashtra NHM routine immunisation page, 17 Apr 2025](https://nhm.maharashtra.gov.in/en/scheme/routine-immunisation-programme/).

**ASHA and NGO complement.** With ANM/AWW, maintain a due list, explain the next visit, remind caregivers and record referral completion against official vaccination documentation. NGO assistance is useful for temporary settlements, migrants, accessible information and continuity across relocation. Vaccination and adverse-event assessment belong to trained authorized health personnel; NGOs can assist navigation back to the health team.

**Bottleneck evidence.** The official health-worker handbook explicitly requires microplanning that includes hamlets, urban subwards, migratory populations and temporary settlements, and tracking dropouts. It demonstrates recognized operational needs, not the local share of missed children. Validate local due lists, session cancellations and coverage before selecting an intervention. [NHM Immunization Handbook for Health Workers, 2018](https://www.nhm.gov.in/New_Updates_2018/NHM_Components/Immunization/Guildelines_for_immunization/Immunization_Handbook_for_Health_Workers-English.pdf) (use here only for planning roles; clinical schedules require newer guidance).

**Overlap/funding.** A dose delivered during MI remains a UIP dose and is counted once. No automatic NGO grant, vaccine-administration authorization or government-data integration entitlement was verified.

### 8. Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana — AB PM-JAY

**Purpose and core benefit.** PM-JAY protects eligible families against large covered hospital expenses: **up to ₹5 lakh per family per year** for secondary/tertiary hospitalization at empanelled public/private hospitals, cashless at the point of service, including specified pre/post-hospitalization expenses. Pre-existing conditions are covered from day one; family size/age/gender limits do not restrict members of an eligible family. This is not ₹5 lakh cash paid to each patient, and routine outpatient care or every private-hospital service is not automatically covered. [MoHFW RML Hospital official key-features page](https://rmlh.mohfw.gov.in/index1.aspx?langid=1&lev=2&lid=3620&lsid=2567) (last updated 5 Feb 2025).

**Eligibility and 70+ expansion.** Verify the household in the official beneficiary system and applicable state-added categories rather than treating poverty, ASHA referral or an ABHA number as sufficient eligibility proof. The 11 September 2024 Cabinet approval expanded eligibility to Indian senior citizens **70+ irrespective of income**. Already-covered families receive an additional up-to-₹5-lakh senior-only annual top-up, not a general ₹10-lakh entitlement for younger members. For other senior families, ₹5 lakh is shared on a family basis. Seniors with CGHS/ECHS/CAPF benefits must choose the applicable public scheme; the official clarification allows seniors with private insurance/ESI to use this expansion. [Cabinet approval, 11 Sep 2024](https://nha.gov.in/img/resources/Press_Release.pdf), [NHA senior-citizen FAQ](https://nha.gov.in/img/resources/English_FAQs_related_to_the_benefits_for_senior_citizens.pdf) (FAQ date unstated; accessed 8 Oct 2026).

**Geography and referral.** NHA sets the national framework, State Health Agencies operate state arrangements, and care is delivered by currently empanelled hospitals with applicable specialties/packages and authorization. **Delhi became the 35th implementing State/UT on 5 April 2025**; older sources excluding Delhi and Odisha are stale. This dated count is not a guarantee of an accessible hospital in every district. Verify participation, hospital empanelment, relevant package and portability before travel. [PIB Delhi implementation update, 5 Apr 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2119281).

**ASHA and NGO complement.** ASHA can help households learn about coverage and obtain a verified referral; NGOs can assist official card/eligibility checks, language access, transport planning and handoff to the hospital's Ayushman help desk. Clinical admission/authorization is decided through the hospital/SHA workflow. The official April 2025 release also reports inclusion of frontline ASHA/Anganwadi workers in health coverage; verify the worker/family record rather than assuming an ID alone activates it.

**Bottleneck/funding.** Incomplete identity verification, a distant suitable empanelled hospital and authorization/document gaps are **operational hypotheses** here, not claimed October 2026 national failure rates. Count completed covered admissions separately from card creation. No NGO grant was verified. A charitable hospital can explore provider empanelment only if it meets current requirements; reimbursement for authorized care is provider payment, not general NGO fundraising.

### Evidence notes and district implementation checklist

1. **Current state exception verified:** PMMVY's August 2026 PIB reply takes precedence over the older FAQ's Odisha exclusion. Telangana onboarding should be rechecked at use. The February 2023 time-limit order describes one ANC check; the currently linked WCD FAQ describes at least two. This report uses the current FAQ for milestone preparation and flags the difference for the administering officer/portal rather than promising approval under the older wording. Scheme benefits and application rules still require local validation.
2. **Do not turn old totals into market size:** No historical launch estimate, cumulative campaign total, visits, card count or hospital admissions is used here as the number of unique October 2026 eligible people. Portal activity totals were deliberately omitted.
3. **Program overlap:** One pregnancy may use JSY, PMMVY, JSSK, PMSMA and SUMAN. Store each scheme's status separately but deduplicate the woman/pregnancy. UIP and MI must share one child/dose history; campaign tags describe how a dose was delivered.
4. **Evidence labels:** Historical CAG findings establish a documented past/local bottleneck; charter standards and documentary requirements establish expected process. Unmeasured local obstacles are labeled hypotheses, not national findings.
5. **Practical first contact:** District Health Society/CMHO or equivalent for NHM maternal services; District Immunization Officer for UIP/MI; state-selected WCD/social-welfare/health PMMVY officer and AWC/ASHA for PMMVY; State Health Agency and empanelled-hospital help desk for PM-JAY. Titles vary by state.
6. **Minimum field directory:** Facility name and geography, service capability, designated session, clinical/administrative contact, referral destination, transport route, applicable eligibility/document checklist, escalation channel, last verified date, and a responsible staff member. A national page cannot replace this district directory.
7. **NGO proposal route:** Agree a narrow problem and outcome—completed high-risk referral, due-dose completion, acknowledged maternity application or resolved denial—with the competent authority. Obtain a written role, scope, permissions, escalation contacts and data-sharing terms. Funding must come from a documented grant/contract/CSR/own-resource arrangement; none of these eight program names itself creates NGO funding eligibility.
8. **Data and beneficiary rights:** Use informed consent, minimal access and authorized records. NGOs should not ask for staff credentials or promise direct integration with government beneficiary systems. Provide assistance without representing the NGO as the sanctioning or clinical authority.

All URLs above are primary Indian government sources. Source dates are given where published; otherwise the date is explicitly recorded as unstated and access was 8 October 2026. Local service performance and approved NGO agreements remain to be verified district by district.

**Caveats that affect the ASHA visit optimiser and local NGO pilot**

- **Separate service ownership from the visit route.** Record the responsible facility/department, referral destination, verified opening hours, service availability, required documents and last verification date. Route optimisation cannot itself guarantee a medicine, diagnostic slot, water connection, ration-card approval or loan. Proposed NGO referral assistance in this document is a local design hypothesis, not evidence of a nationwide programme failure.
- **Do not add overlapping benefits/populations.** AAM includes medicines and diagnostics supported by the two NHM initiatives; the same household can also receive NFSA foodgrains and Anganwadi services. NFSA's statutory nutrition provisions overlap with Anganwadi delivery. Count the eight programmes once each, then deduplicate people/households and service events in impact metrics. ONORC and the components subsumed in POSHAN 2.0 do not add extra rows.
- **Funding follows a different decision path.** Household service entitlement, an SHG bank loan, an NGO implementation contract, and a licensed pharmacy's operator incentive are different. The programme list does not identify an unrestricted funding source for a local NGO. Validate actual state empanelment, procurement, grant or operator rules before assigning funds or describing a funded partnership.
- **The latest POSHAN evidence reveals an existing scheduler.** The official **14 April 2026** backgrounder reports an **AWW Home Visit Scheduler integrated into Poshan Tracker in April 2026**, with **23 structured visits** for pregnant women/children up to three, automatic scheduling and counselling/video prompts. This is material prior art for a visit-optimiser proposal, while AWW and ASHA workflows remain distinct. Do not claim that frontline-worker home-visit scheduling does not exist. [PIB current POSHAN backgrounder](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2251769).
- **Use newer policy for current numbers.** The NHM programme page contains **31 August 2018** diagnostic rollout counts and old illustrative test lists; those are not October 2026 status. The cited **1 August 2025** reply gives 14 sub-centre/63 PHC tests. Likewise, the POSHAN baseline approval period ends in 2025–26, but the 2026–27 Budget and April 2026 government description show ongoing programme activity; do not infer that it ended. [NHM baseline page](https://nhm.gov.in/index1.php?lang=1&level=2&sublinkid=1218&lid=192), [2025 reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2151239), [2026–27 Budget](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/eb/vol1.pdf).
- **JJM evidence limitation:** the [new JJM 2.0 English guidelines](https://jaljeevanmission.gov.in/sites/default/files/guideline/Operational-Guidelines-JJM-2.pdf) were identified in the official index but could not be fully extracted because the PDF was too large for the browsing tool and the alternate extractor failed. A historical state example is not a current nationwide mandate.
- **Use the correct NFSA grievance channel.** The central portal's [online grievance utility](https://nfsa.gov.in/public/frmRegisterPublicGrievance.aspx) says submitted input **does not constitute a complaint**. A pilot should verify the state's formal complaint route/District Grievance Redressal Officer rather than promise legal redress through that feedback form. [NFSA responsibilities](https://www.nfsa.gov.in/portal/NFSA-Act).

### Where a national-to-local service network could break

The following are **failure hypotheses to test**, except where an explicit documented dependency is identified. They should not be reported as proven nationwide failures of the platforms.

| Handoff | Potential break and why it matters | Concrete validation / design requirement |
|---|---|---|
| National NGO identity → district service listing | Legal identity, a health facility registration or a donor commitment does not establish that a particular district office supplies a specific service today. | Verify district contact, service eligibility, geographic coverage, staff qualifications where relevant, delivery mode, charges and a renewal date. Keep organisation verification separate from service availability. |
| ASHA due task → appropriate referral | A clinical due-list or consultation can identify need without naming an accessible nonclinical provider. An app may already support referral forms, but the recipient still needs to be connected operationally. | Test the actual beneficiary journey for transport, food assistance, disability support or other chosen services; identify who can refer, what data they see and who is responsible for follow-up. |
| Directory match → accepted slot | A listing can be accurate while the NGO is full, outside catchment, unable to fund the service or unwilling to accept a case. | Require explicit acceptance/decline, current capacity, response deadline, queue ownership and an alternative provider/escalation. Measure unanswered referrals, not just referral counts. |
| Consent → appropriate data sharing | ABDM consented health-record exchange and Ni-kshay's support consent are documented, but purpose, roles and data minimization differ for social support. | Record service-specific consent; share only necessary referral data with the accepting NGO; maintain an assisted route and withdrawal handling. Reuse existing official exchange only with authorized access. |
| Offline task → consistent shared state | ANMOL/AMRIT/CommCare/U-WIN already support offline use. Offline functionality does not ensure that a second provider sees newly accepted/completed tasks before synchronization. | Test sync latency, duplicate identities, conflicting updates and failed delivery under real district connectivity. Do not advertise offline as a unique new feature. |
| Accepted referral → completed service | Consultation, reported dose, recorded support count and actual beneficiary experience are different outcomes. Ni-kshay already prescribes follow-up confirmation, showing the importance of this step. | Record accepted, scheduled, attempted, delivered, beneficiary-confirmed, declined and failed states; use proportionate evidence, independent sample checks and a complaint route. |
| Completion → ASHA/supervisor accountability | App dashboards can track activities while unresolved cross-organisation referrals have no clear owner. Historical CommCare field evidence shows supervision did not automatically improve with digital tools. | Assign a named case owner and supervisor escalation, return concise outcomes to the ASHA, and monitor unresolved time and beneficiary-reported delivery alongside entries. |
| National scale → sustainable district operation | Software, state deployment, training and physical service capacity must all be funded. Existing pilots/implementation documents do not prove uniform scale or lasting capacity. | Pilot a few services in two contrasting districts; cost case coordination and field verification; obtain district/state workflow ownership; document minimum staffing and financing before scale. |

### What this means for an ASHA visit optimizer

**Proposed pilot approach:** use Darpan for identity cross-checking, GuideStar India and Give Discover for a shortlist, and direct partner onboarding to establish service availability. Approach iVolunteer/ConnectFor for suitable volunteer support and Goodera for scheduled corporate participation or in-kind work. Treat Give, GlobalGiving and relevant NGOBOX calls as longer-term program/funding channels, with eligible nonprofit partners and explicit budgets.

Keep organizational discovery separate from operational matching. An organization's registered office, pincode, district label or national presence claim is not its actual beneficiary catchment. A directory total is not the number of active organizations available to support one village this week. GuideStar India, iVolunteer and Give pages have multiple historical counts; this report deliberately avoids turning them into an addressable active-service estimate.

**Pilot directory fields to validate with each partner:** legal name/identifiers; evidence source and certification expiry; service category; covered villages/blocks; referral eligibility; exclusions; current staff/stock/budget; response window; contact owner; accessibility/languages; referral route; acceptance/decline reason; next availability date; and completion feedback. These are proposed product requirements, not features claimed to exist on every platform.

An ASHA-recorded request for food, accompaniment, disability support or entitlement assistance should reach a partner only after a coordinator checks suitability and the resident consents to necessary sharing. Aggregated needs can inform a donor proposal without disclosing patient names publicly. Clinical urgency must continue through the appropriate health-system pathway; a fundraising or volunteering search is not an emergency response mechanism.

### Evidence distinctions and corrections for synthesis

- **Darpan identity is not endorsement.** The portal's notice expressly denies endorsement, affiliation and recognition; do not label an NGO “NITI approved.” Its signup guide describes document checks for ID generation, which should not be translated into impact certification. [Notice](https://ngodarpan.gov.in/?lang=de), [official signup guide](https://ngodarpan.gov.in/assets/ngo_doc/NGO_Darpan_How_to_Signup_English.pdf).
- **Verification differs by level and content.** GuideStar certification is separate from its explicitly unvetted user announcements. Give transparency stars describe profile completeness, while diligence seals describe checks. A historical badge or listing must be checked for current validity. [GuideStar](https://www.guidestarindia.org/), [Give standards](https://discover.give.do/news/give-launches-guaranteed-trust-seals/).
- **Funding is conditional.** Access to donors, grant listings, CSR buyers or a fundraising account does not guarantee funds, unrestricted spending, medical reimbursement or fast disbursement. GlobalGiving does document unrestricted fundraising as an option; do not overcorrect by claiming all platform funding is restricted. [GlobalGiving explanation](https://www.globalgiving.org/learn/how-globalgiving-vets-nonprofits/).
- **Two APIs are documented; one is announced as future.** GlobalGiving and Goodera have official API documentation. GuideStar India's homepage says “Coming soon.” API access for the remaining five was not verified, rather than proven absent. No authenticated functionality, pricing, SLA or production access was tested.
- **Operators must be named accurately.** Current Give platform terms identify SaathiRe; NGOBOX's current footer identifies Renalysis Consultants; GuideStar India is CSISI; iVolunteer is MITRA's program; ConnectFor is Rosy Blue Foundation's program. Historical GiveIndia legal documents should not override current platform terms.
- **Capacity and workflow gaps remain hypotheses.** None of the reviewed public pages proves a ready live directory of rural health-support capacity integrated with ASHA schedules. This supports testing a coordinator-managed referral layer; it does not establish that existing platforms cannot add or already privately provide one.

### Design implications for the ASHA + local NGO assistance layer

These are **design hypotheses to test locally**, not findings that every existing service fails:

- Separate the beneficiary's record into scheme candidates, applications, official decisions, NGO service commitments, corporate proposals, donations received and verified expenditure. A discovery match, CSR expression of interest and published fundraiser represent different stages.
- Give each referral an owner, receiving organization/contact, requested action, permission record, acknowledgement, expected next update and escalation route. The public sources reviewed do not establish one shared district referral contract across these eight services.
- Start with linked official pages and receipt-based reconciliation. Pursue a Haqdarshak commercial API or government integration only after scope, access rights, security responsibilities and permitted data exchange are agreed; avoid inferring access from an app's internal APIs.
- For Rajasthan, make kiosk charges visible and distinguish service fees from departmental fees. Elsewhere, identify the appropriate local assisted-service channel rather than importing Rajasthan eMitra as a national network.
- Keep a district NGO directory independently checked for service category, operating area, present capacity and eligibility. CSR portal registration, campaign presence and historical spending are insufficient evidence of an NGO's ability to accept a particular referral today.
- Use aggregate, documented unmet needs for possible CSR projects. The government release and Exchange FAQ describe company-board control; neither creates a pooled cash balance that the optimizer can allocate. Crowdfunding may supplement a documented residual need but donor response and net payout remain uncertain.
- Clinical urgency and ASHA visit priorities need their own authorized health workflow. None of the reviewed documentation established an end-to-end clinical triage, route optimization, NGO acceptance and funding approval engine. This is a bounded evidence gap to validate through product demonstrations and a district pilot, not proof that such private integrations do not exist.

### Key corrections for synthesis

**myScheme ≠ sanction; UMANG availability ≠ department approval; Rajasthan eMitra ≠ nationwide/free assisted delivery; CSR disclosures ≠ available grants; CSR Exchange ≠ pooled cash or guaranteed funding; Haqdarshak ≠ government authority and is more than a discovery-only website; 0% crowdfunding platform fee ≠ zero total deductions; published crowdfunding campaign ≠ donations or immediate payout.**

No claim of an open API should be made for six services where public documentation was not verified. For **UMANG**, distinguish official integration architecture from access authorization. For **Haqdarshak**, distinguish an operator's commercial SaaS/API offer from published endpoint documentation. Across all eight, absence of public verification is a research limit, not evidence that no private integration exists.

### Three service cases to validate

These are synthetic operating scenarios, not real beneficiaries, secured partners or promised benefits. No clinical thresholds, drug instructions or helpline numbers are included.

#### A. Rural pregnancy follow-up with a transport barrier

The health team has already marked a household due for follow-up. The optimizer includes the remote hamlet despite extra travel. At the visit, the person asks for help reaching the approved facility. ASHA records the barrier and permitted contact once; ANM/CHO handles any clinical concern through the existing route. Coordinator first checks the applicable public transport/service entitlement and actual booking channel, including JSSK where eligible; it is not a universal entitlement for every journey. If unavailable, the public-service failure stays open while an opted-in NGO is asked about a bridge ride.

The NGO office may be near the PHC but serve a different block, so it fails the catchment filter. Another offer needs an accessible vehicle, confirmed facility slot and return plan; its last confirmed capacity has expired, so coordinator reconfirms before promising the ride. If the person declines accompaniment, record the choice and offer permitted alternatives. **Validate:** completed facility attendance, public/NGO transport separately, actual household cost, essential visit coverage and ASHA/coordinator minutes. No NGO confirmation closes the medical follow-up.

#### B. Rural child referred for assessment/rehabilitation

An existing RBSK/clinical pathway identifies a child needing assessment. The caregiver can make one long journey but cannot afford repeated weekly travel or missed wages. The official service/DEIC confirms current eligibility, staffing, appointments and any onward pathway; the app must not copy an old condition list or fabricate a local rehabilitation provider. Coordinator checks public treatment, rehabilitation/device channels and authorized agency requirements before arranging NGO logistics.

A nearby “disability NGO” offers document navigation but no qualified therapy; it can accept that distinct request, not substitute for clinical assessment. For ADIP/RVY or camps, verify current authorization, appropriate assessment, local dates and funded device availability; portal presence is insufficient. A provider without home service or sustainable repeat capacity is not a complete solution. Caregiver has no smartphone: worker/coordinator provides an assisted status slip and private agreed follow-up. **Validate:** assessment attended, onward service initiated and sustained where required, journeys/wage loss, reasons for discontinuation, support refusal and extra coordination time. S3 supports investigating these barriers, not predicting this family's outcome.

#### C. Urban migrant household already under the TB programme

A consenting household in a vulnerable ward reports interrupted food support and has moved within the city. Care and support continue through the authorized NTEP/TB unit and Ni-kshay pathway; a new generic app does not duplicate TB registration. With permitted data, coordinator verifies current ward/contact, DTO/TU assignment, Ni-kshay Mitra linkage and actual basket delivery. Nutrition-payment state and food-support state remain separate. For other household needs, existing ration/ICDS/social-welfare channels are checked before supplementary NGO help.

S1 already supplies district-supervised responsibilities; reuse them. A local Mitra/NGO confirms whether the new ward is covered and whether the upcoming delivery has stock. An old donation pledge is insufficient. A public case map, diagnosis-bearing SMS or photograph publicity is unacceptable; offer a recipient-chosen discreet delivery/contact method. If support is declined or consent withdrawn, stop the voluntary support contact as agreed while preserving lawful programme care. **Validate:** authorized transfer/linkage acknowledged, confirmed delivery/quality, unresolved rounds, recipient privacy experience and coordinator effort. Do not interpret a delivered basket as treatment completion.

### Minimal operating agreement and pilot decision

Before opening intake, the district/implementation partner and participating providers should agree: **who coordinates and funds the work; which households/catchments are included; who approves clinical/entitlement decisions; which services are actually accepting cases; update/response arrangements; minimal data/consent; backup and grievance route; and permissible completion evidence**. This is an H pilot recommendation, not a newly asserted statutory requirement. For TB, align with the established DTO/TU structure rather than substituting a private coordinator for programme authority.

A service record should include `branch`, `service`, `covered_localities`, `eligibility_version`, `cost`, `accessibility`, `intake_hours`, `contact_channel`, `capacity_quantity`, `capacity_confirmed_at`, `valid_until`, `accepting_intake`, `responsible_person`. A need needs `consent_scope`, `safe_contact`, `case_owner`, `backup`, `next_action_at`, `public_pathway_status` and separate support/clinical status. “Unknown” is an explicit state. Match by these service facts before distance; a nation-level registration total never supplies local capacity.

Start with one willing primary-care team and a few locally confirmed offers; the exact size is a proposal to fit local coordination resources. First trace recent successful, failed and refused cases across all eleven handoffs. Then run the planner/support workflow for a defined cohort and compare with the existing process. **Burden neutrality is an acceptance criterion to test**, not a claim: no net increase in ASHA administrative/chasing time and no reduction in essential due coverage. Coordinator/provider effort is measured separately rather than hidden as free labour.

Keep denominators honest: eligible due visits, actually attempted/completed visits, unique consented support cases, offers, accepted slots, reported delivery, recipient-confirmed delivery and unresolved/withdrawn cases. Report time to resolution and reasons for failure, including no capacity, inaccessible travel, public entitlement delay and refusal. Include remote/migrant/no-smartphone households and disclose missing confirmation rather than excluding them. Measure recipient cost, privacy incidents and complaints. These operational results cannot establish causal health benefits without a suitable evaluation.

The best first demonstration is a **failed match that is visibly recovered by its owner**, while a remote due visit stays protected. If a paper/phone pilot cannot identify real capacity and an accountable receiving person, a wider digital directory should wait.

# Part 10. Proposed solution and operating model

## ASHA Saath: proposed solution and operating model

Working name only; brand uniqueness has not been researched. Research date: 8 October 2026. All architecture, examples, targets and budgets below are proposals, not deployed capabilities or measured impact.

### Recommendation

Build a visit planner with a small support-referral layer: **help the worker decide whom to visit today, then track whether a need discovered during that visit reaches an eligible, available provider and is actually addressed.** Keep government services, clinical care and NGO support as distinguishable pathways within the same need record.

The biggest implementation constraint is partner operations. Software cannot create transport, nutrition supplies, qualified staff or unrestricted funds. The minimum viable network is a willing local coordinator and a few providers with specific, current service offers. A national catalogue can help discovery later; national participation does not mean every NGO serves every village.

This is an adaptation hypothesis, not a first-ever invention. Indian frontline tools already support workplans and offline capture; overseas social-care platforms already document referral outcome tracking. The useful combination to validate is ASHA visit planning, Indian entitlement navigation, actual local service catchments, freshness of capacity, and follow-through with very little additional worker entry. See the platform catalogue and primary product documentation for the comparison.

### Stay inside Healthcare 03

The original problem is **Community Health Worker (ASHA/ANM) Visit Scheduling Optimizer with Risk-Weighted Prioritization**. Its expanded requirements, read earlier in this session, cover caseload CSV ingestion, geography-based daily routes, rule-based priority, travel estimates, risk flags and coverage projection. Current extraction reconfirmed the title but did not expand the description. [Official problem statements](https://hackathon.gitjaipur.com/ps).

The NGO feature should start **after a visit** and feed relevant follow-up tasks back into the planner. A stand-alone donation website would leave the required scheduling problem unsolved. Do not add NGO financing urgency to a clinical risk score: medical priority and support-access urgency are separate fields with separate owners.

### Four journeys that make the idea concrete

1. **Pregnancy follow-up and access barrier:** an ASHA visits a person already marked for priority follow-up by the health team. The person reports a transport or documentation problem. Show the applicable public-service pathway and local contact first. If the public service cannot be accessed in time, record the barrier and ask an opted-in provider whether a permitted bridge service is available. An NGO referral does not close the original medical task. A public-service failure remains visible to the supervisor even if temporary help is arranged.
2. **Child nutrition support:** route the assessment and programme follow-up to the authorized health/Anganwadi team. With consent, a non-clinical family-support need may be offered to a provider with suitable local services. Dietary treatment and clinical decisions remain with qualified staff; a food parcel is not proof that a clinical condition has resolved.
3. **Assistive-device access:** the worker identifies that an older or disabled person wants help navigating an assessment, certificate, device camp or transport. Match an eligibility navigator and a provider's actual programme catchment. Record separate milestones for assessment, application and device delivery; a successful form submission is not device receipt.
4. **TB-related social support:** only use an authorized and consented pathway aligned with the programme. Nutrition or livelihood assistance may complement treatment services. Keep sensitive identity and diagnosis out of donor listings. Support delivered is not treatment completion. Programme and beneficiary overlap with existing TB systems must be reconciled in a real pilot.

These are hypothetical journeys. No named beneficiary, local partnership or current service availability is asserted.

### Network design from village to national level

| Level | Who operates it | Data and decision | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Household / village / urban ward | ASHA and authorized health team | Today’s visits, consented need, preferred contact and constraints | Worker sees assigned cases; household identity stays restricted |
| PHC / urban primary health team | Supervisor or designated coordinator | Review priority, handle failed service access, follow unresolved needs | Clinical escalation stays in the health chain |
| Block / municipality / district | Named network coordinator with participating NGOs | Validate offers, maintain service catchments, contact non-responsive providers | Operational accountability must be agreed, not assumed |
| State | Authorized programme administrators / implementation partner | Maintain state-specific programme rules and aggregate capacity gaps | State eligibility and rollout cannot be copied from another state |
| National | Discovery catalogue and approved programme/funder interfaces | Search registered organizations and national programmes | Provider office location is not proof of nationwide delivery |

**Geography belongs to a service offer, not just to an organization.** A Jaipur-based NGO might serve two rural blocks for nutrition, provide counselling remotely in Rajasthan, and run a device camp only on one date. Those are three different offers. An organization marked “national” must still declare the catchment and eligibility of each offer.

Urban matching must handle wards, informal settlements, temporary addresses, migration and contact safety. Rural matching must handle hamlets, travel time, roads, outreach days and last-mile delivery. PIN codes help discovery but are often too coarse to establish eligibility or a feasible route. Keep geography configurable and validate it locally.

### Minimum information for a service offer

- Organization and branch identifiers; authorized service contact; legal identity evidence and review date.
- Service category and plain-language description; clinical/non-clinical boundary; who may deliver it.
- Catchment expressed as selected administrative units and, where needed, boundaries; delivery mode and accessibility.
- Eligibility conditions, exclusions, required documents and rule source/version.
- Opening times, intake process, response expectation and whether the offer is open, waitlisted or paused.
- Capacity quantity and unit (for example appointments this week, not an unspecified number); last confirmation and expiry.
- Cost, grant restrictions and whether assistance is free, partly funded or requires approval.
- Outcome states and allowed delivery evidence; grievance contact and re-verification date.

Registration, service readiness and funding authorization are three separate checks. NGO Darpan supplies a registration ID, with an explicit disclaimer that the ID is not an endorsement. A “verified” badge should explain exactly which checks were performed rather than imply government approval. [NPO Darpan](https://ngodarpan.gov.in/?lang=de).

### Need capture that does not burden the worker

Proposed first version: after marking a visit, the worker selects a support category, confirms that the person wants assistance, notes locality and the preferred safe contact method, and chooses the observed access barrier. Reuse authorized data already in the caseload rather than asking for another full registration form. Free text is optional. Voice entry may produce a draft the worker must confirm.

Capture only what is needed for the requested service. Full clinical history, Aadhaar scans, family photographs and diagnoses are not default NGO intake fields. A case can be handled with an internal identifier; the provider receives only the fields authorized for that referral. A person without a smartphone or OTP capability must have an assisted pathway. Missing identity documents should create a navigation task where possible rather than an automatic permanent rejection.

Separate refusal from unreachable status. Record “declined support” respectfully and stop repeated contact according to the person's preference. Household health consent is not blanket consent for every NGO, donor or future service.

### Referral states and accountability

Suggested states:

`draft -> consent recorded -> eligible offer selected -> offered -> accepted -> appointment/service arranged -> provider reports delivered -> beneficiary/worker confirmation -> closed`

Side branches must support `ineligible`, `declined by provider`, `expired/no response`, `waitlisted`, `person declined`, `cancelled`, `consent withdrawn`, `delivery disputed` and `closed unresolved`.

“Offered” means a provider was asked; “accepted” means the provider agreed; “delivered” means the service was reported as supplied; “confirmed” means the agreed verification was recorded. Reports show each stage separately. Closing unresolved preserves the failure reason; it must not be counted as a successful resolution.

Each case has a named follow-up owner and a next action date. The implementation partner must budget and assign coordinator time; this responsibility must not silently become unpaid ASHA work. A district coordinator can review provider non-response and offer alternatives with appropriate permission. Escalation intervals depend on the service and are locally agreed; they are not universal clinical deadlines. Urgent clinical situations use the established health referral process immediately and do not wait for an NGO timeout timer.

In a real deployment, one service request can generate several offers, but only the agreed active allocation reserves a limited slot. Use transaction-safe reservations with expiry; release the reservation on cancellation. Avoid broadcasting identifiable cases to every NGO. Administrative duplicates can be reviewed without denying legitimate repeat needs.

### Matching that can be explained

First apply hard filters: service fit, eligibility known or requiring review, verified provider readiness, true catchment, accessible delivery modality, open intake, valid capacity and beneficiary consent. Uncertainty goes to manual review. Do not manufacture an eligible match when no offer passes.

Then rank the remaining candidates using understandable factors: earliest feasible service, recipient's preference, travel/access burden, freshness of capacity, response history with enough sample context, and the requested support deadline. Show a short explanation: “serves this ward; document help available Tuesday; capacity confirmed today.” A new NGO should not be permanently excluded because it has little platform history. Paying for promotion must not control vulnerable households' service ranking.

Clinical risk and affordability are not monetized lead-generation scores. Each suggested service has an accept/decline decision by the responsible human. Government eligibility is “potentially eligible” until the responsible authority confirms it; the application or benefit approval is not automated by an LLM.

### Visit optimization

Use staff-supplied or approved rule-based priority, due dates, visit durations, household availability, location confidence, shift length and start/end location. Preserve the PS's geographic step: use basic geographic grouping or the stated nearest-neighbour approach, then generate a priority-aware route within the shift. Clearly name the implemented method in the demo. Advanced clustering, search or OR-Tools can come later if the team has time.

Optimize coverage subject to feasibility, then reduce unnecessary travel within the accepted coverage plan. A remote priority visit may legitimately increase travel. Show the tradeoff and all unserved visits, with reasons: shift full, location missing, household unavailable or requires supervisor review. Coordinate confidence and route assumptions must be visible. Straight-line distance multiplied by an assumed speed is an estimate, not a road ETA.

Repeatedly missed or remote households need an explicit coverage safeguard. Test a maximum permissible postponement policy approved by the supervisor rather than allowing a distance-first algorithm to keep deferring them. Do not claim a mathematically optimal route unless the algorithm and solver status support that claim.

Support follow-up tasks consume worker/coordinator time too. A proposed NGO appointment should not silently fill an already infeasible ASHA day. Multi-worker reallocation requires authorization and realistic travel areas, rather than assuming any worker can cover any household.

### Government service access before substituting donations

The scheme catalogue informs an entitlement checklist. Its minimal record contains programme, state applicability, beneficiary rules, support type, responsible facility/department, application or service channel, evidence link, effective date and last review. Include public services that need no conventional “application,” not just cash transfers.

“Available on a website” is not equivalent to “accessible to this household.” Record the actual barrier: unaware of benefit, eligibility unclear, documents missing, application pending, facility unreachable, stock unavailable, no response or person chose another option. An NGO bridge may help but should not erase the public-service gap. A scheduled review keeps rules from becoming stale.

Do not automatically merge related scheme amounts. For example, public delivery benefits, maternity transfers and nutrition programmes have different rules; one person may access several while still being only one beneficiary. Programmes/campaigns and entitlements are classified distinctly in the catalogue.

### Funding and in-kind support

### Deterministic planning, with clinical boundaries

The software schedules **already classified tasks**. It neither diagnoses disease nor generates clinical risk from symptoms, photographs or an LLM. Imported risk flags show their source, owner and date. Unknown or contradictory classification is a review task. Incentive amounts, donor interest and fundraising urgency never alter clinical priority.

An urgent flag from an approved pathway creates **clinical escalation required** before routine route generation. Display the locally configured escalation contact/instruction and record acknowledgment and handoff. The worker can also invoke that pathway when a finding warrants escalation under her training. The planner cannot declare the situation safe, replace assessment or wait for NGO acceptance. The urgent task remains visible until an authorized health-team disposition; it is excluded from routine-route coverage with a separate escalation count, rather than shown as a successfully completed visit. ASHA referral and trained cadre boundaries are grounded in NHM/NHSRC guidance. [NHM ASHA role](https://www.nhm.gov.in/images/index1.php?lang=1&level=1&lid=226&sublinkid=150), [NHSRC Community Processes](https://nhsrcindia.org/practice-areas/cpc-phc/community-process-asha).

Proposed planning procedure:

1. Freeze an input snapshot and policy version for a selected staff member/date. Respect existing staff assignment and required cadre. Split tasks into escalation, review, eligible due, future and completed groups. Joint visits are deferred in the MVP: a task marked joint-required is visibly `coordination_required` and cannot be counted as independently completed by one cadre.
2. Perform required **geography-based grouping by confirmed locality**: tasks sharing a confirmed village/hamlet/ward locality ID form a group; tasks without a confirmed locality retain individual ungrouped markers rather than an invented common catchment. Retain group membership in planning output and show group-level coverage. Within an otherwise identical priority/deadline/deferral/due-date tier, construct a deterministic nearest-neighbour seed order from the staff start coordinate, repeatedly choosing the closest remaining task and using task ID to break distance ties. Geographic grouping and this route seed are explicit MVP outputs, not optional enhancements.
3. Order eligible due tasks by approved priority bucket, then approved latest permissible date, then deferral count descending, due date, and the nearest-neighbour seed order only within those ties. Geography cannot override those approved service/fairness fields. A supervisor-configured maximum postponement policy raises a visible mandatory-review constraint when exceeded; do not invent a universal medical interval or change clinical priority merely because a case is remote. Display groups and distance tie-breaking cannot justify repeatedly deferring a remote household without this visible review.
4. Insert tasks one at a time in that deterministic order. For each task, evaluate every insertion position in the current route. Recompute travel, arrival, waiting, visit duration, fixed commitments, breaks, buffer and return-to-end time. Choose the feasible insertion with the lowest incremental travel; tie-break on earliest arrival, then insertion position. If none is feasible, retain a named unserved reason.
5. Optionally apply a deterministic travel-reduction pass only to the selected visit set, preserving priority coverage, availability and commitments. This is a heuristic feasible plan, not a mathematical optimum.
6. Persist route version, selected IDs, timings, assumptions and all rejected/review IDs. Manual edits trigger the same feasibility check. A coordinator may authorize an override with a reason; it does not erase a warning or make an impossible shift feasible.

The primary objective is approved priority and due-service coverage within a usable day; travel is a secondary choice within that coverage plan. A remote priority visit may increase kilometres and reduce total visit count. Show that tradeoff. If a high-priority task cannot fit even alone, give it a specific escalation to supervisor review; do not silently fill the day with easier tasks and claim all needs covered.

For a first build, compute a deterministic pairwise distance estimate using coordinates and a declared mode/speed assumption, optionally a separately calibrated detour factor. Label it **estimated travel from coordinates, not a road ETA**. Add service time, return travel and waiting to the time budget. Missing coordinates, a blocked road reported by the worker, an unavailable household or uncertain access require manual review or explicit correction. A haversine estimate cannot prove a traversable route. Show a confidence badge and last location confirmation. Road matrices and traffic can come later after access, cost and routing quality are evaluated.

#### Coverage and fairness output

Show due eligible visits covered/total, bucket-level coverage, planned travel/service/wait/buffer minutes, planned end time and all unserved cases with reason and next owner. Separately list escalations, review cases, joint-required cases and future tasks so denominator changes cannot hide them. Show remote/locality coverage and consecutive deferrals. Avoid a public household map or a punitive worker GPS score.

For the required **coverage projection**, rerun the same scheduler across the next three configured working days on the frozen task backlog, removing simulated scheduled tasks from subsequent simulated days and advancing the **planning date**, while preserving each task's approved due date/window. An authorized source update may change a due date through a separate reviewed event; the projection does not move deadlines. Respect each day's known availability. State assumptions: no new cases, no cancellations, the same travel model and supplied future household windows. Unknown future availability is marked unconfirmed; remaining unscheduled tasks remain visible. This is forecast coverage under assumptions, not a promise of visits or a health outcome.

### One NGO flow: consented document-navigation assistance

After completing a visit, the worker chooses `document_navigation`, records the actual access barrier and public pathway already tried, confirms that assistance is wanted, and selects a safe contact preference. Reuse internal case/locality references; do not copy full health records. The new support task has its own coordinator owner and next-action date. The health task remains governed by its own completion/clinical state.

A **service offer** belongs to a branch and a specific service, not to the organisation's national address. It declares confirmed villages/wards or administrative IDs, delivery mode/accessibility, eligibility and exclusions, source/version, opening times, response deadline, cost/free status, grant restriction if any, current capacity unit/window, confirmation timestamp, expiry and responsible operator. A registered NGO is not automatically a qualified or available provider. Darpan's ID explicitly does not confer government endorsement. [NPO Darpan notice](https://ngodarpan.gov.in/?lang=de).

Matching uses hard filters first: service category, actual catchment, readiness check still valid, eligibility confirmed for the navigation service, accessible modality, open intake window, unexpired capacity and permitted consent purpose. Unknown eligibility is `manual_review`; stale capacity is `capacity_reconfirmation_required`; no eligible offer yields `unmatched`, never an invented match. Eligibility to receive document help is distinct from eligibility for the underlying government benefit.

Rank only passing offers by earliest feasible appointment, beneficiary preference, accessibility/travel burden, capacity freshness, then stable offer ID. Explain each match in plain language. For the demonstration, offer one case to one provider at a time. The case may have sequential attempts when an offer is declined or expires. Changing providers requires that check again. Acceptance cannot enlarge the consented field set.

#### State machine and permission to move state

The need and individual offer attempts are separate records; a failed attempt does not erase the need.

| State | Allowed next action and owner | What the state proves |
|---|---|---|
| `draft` | Worker records category, barrier and owner; person may decline | A need draft exists; no sharing |
| `consent_pending` | Assisted or direct service-specific permission recorded; refusal closes as `person_declined` | Permission has not yet been granted |
| `ready_for_match` | Coordinator/system evaluates current offers and eligibility | Permission permits the selected purpose; no provider commitment |
| `manual_review` / `unmatched` | Coordinator resolves uncertainty or confirms another offer, with current permission | No valid allocation; remains on unresolved dashboard |
| `offered` | Identified provider accepts/declines before deadline; coordinator may cancel; system may expire | Provider has been asked, not booked |
| `accepted` | Provider reserves capacity atomically and sets appointment; person may decline; coordinator can cancel | Provider agreed to the bounded service |
| `arranged` | Provider records appointment/access plan; missed attempt creates follow-up | A service arrangement, not delivery |
| `delivery_reported` | Provider records completed navigation session and next-step outcome; authorized worker/person confirms or disputes | Provider says the session occurred |
| `confirmed` | Worker/person records agreed proportionate confirmation; coordinator closes | Confirmation of document-help session only |
| `closed_resolved` | Terminal; correction requires auditable reopening | This support service was confirmed, not benefit approval or improved health |
| `delivery_disputed` | Coordinator investigates, then resolves or closes unresolved | Conflict remains visible; not counted as confirmed |
| `closed_unresolved` | Coordinator records reason and remaining public-service barrier | Closure without successful support |
| `consent_withdrawn` / `cancelled` / `person_declined` | Stop new sharing/arrangements, release allocation, record contact preference | No further permitted active referral under that consent |

Offer attempts have their own terminal `provider_declined`, `expired` and `cancelled` states. A waitlist is an explicit follow-up branch with consent, owner and review date; it cannot be displayed as accepted capacity. Decline reasons use bounded categories, with optional minimized text. Recontact after refusal follows the person's preference; do not equate refusal with unreachable status.

Use separate `service_outcome` values such as `checklist_explained`, `application_step_prepared`, `referred_to_authority` and `next_step_unresolved`. A completed navigation session can coexist with an unresolved entitlement barrier. Completion of the health visit, acceptance of NGO help, service reporting, beneficiary confirmation and public-benefit approval remain distinct events.

#### Acceptance and conflict transaction

Provider acceptance is an online server transaction, never a client-only button. Lock the need, current consent and relevant offer/capacity row in a consistent order. Recheck provider/assignment permission, state/version, consent still active, confirmed eligibility, catchment, valid capacity window, offer expiry and any required funding authorization. Atomically reserve one unit only if available, create the allocation, and transition the attempt/need to accepted. Commit together; failure leaves no reservation. Add database constraints for one active allocation per need/service request and nonnegative capacity.

Every mutating request includes stable event ID, actor, client timestamp and expected record version. Duplicate event IDs return the stored result without a second allocation or visit count. A different event against a stale version returns an explicit conflict and current authoritative state. Two provider acceptance requests for one remaining slot cannot both succeed; the loser receives `capacity_unavailable` or `allocation_conflict` and is sent for coordinator review. An expiry/cancellation race uses the same locks and state checks. Release capacity exactly once on cancellation or withdrawal; consumption after delivered service remains a historical fact, not a new free slot in the same accounting window.

Capacity is accounted as `total = available + active reservations + consumed` for the declared service window, with any authorised adjustment separately logged. A reservation expiry before arrangement releases its unit and returns the need to `manual_review` with `capacity_reconfirmation_required`; the UI must remove the booking promise. An arranged appointment uses the provider-confirmed appointment window and its cancellation policy, rather than silently expiring on a short offer-response timer. Delivered service atomically changes reserved to consumed once.

Withdrawal increments consent version and marks the case blocked for new sharing in the same transaction, cancels eligible active offers/arrangements, and releases unconsumed reservations. Notify the responsible provider through the product and log acknowledgment. It cannot recall information already legitimately viewed or undo completed service; retention/deletion follows the agreed policy. Late delivery evidence after withdrawal is quarantined for coordinator review, with factual chronology retained; it cannot silently reopen the case or imply renewed consent. Provider-reported delivery and beneficiary dispute are two append-only facts rather than last-write-wins edits.

### Offline: a limited, honest promise

For the hackathon, cache only the synthetic assigned day's plan and minimal task references. A worker can read it and queue visit completion or a need draft with UUID event IDs; display **offline, pending server acknowledgment** and last-sync time. Downloaded matches are explicitly stale observations. Offline users cannot reserve shared NGO capacity, confirm provider acceptance, change approved clinical rules or finalize fund approval. An appointment slot becomes real only after online acceptance or a separately logged authorized coordinator channel.

Record local withdrawal immediately, stop further local sharing and prioritize its sync. Do not promise instantaneous revocation on another offline device. On reconnection, authenticate anew, recheck role/assignment and consent version, replay duplicate-safe events and show conflicts rather than merging clinically meaningful facts automatically. Server revocation rejects queued prohibited changes. Keep a visible pending/conflict count and allow safe correction; no hidden “sync successful” if some records failed. A production offline implementation needs device controls, encrypted protected storage, expiry, loss handling, secure deletion and a defined revocation delay. A service worker caching synthetic data does not establish production privacy readiness.

Minimal interface contracts: import/validate CSV; generate/read plan snapshot; submit visit event; create need/record consent; list explainable matches; send offer; accept/decline; arrange/report/confirm/dispute; withdraw consent; read coordinator unresolved queue. Server handlers enforce explicit transitions instead of letting clients set arbitrary status values. Rate limits, secure sessions, database constraints and ordinary error handling belong in the same backend. The exact database library and frontend framework are routine implementation choices, not distinguishing product features.

### Access and data minimization

Worker access is limited to assigned cases. Supervisors see authorized geography; clinicians own clinical priority and health escalation. Providers see only requests offered to their service and fields consented for intake, with contact details released only at the agreed step. A provider cannot browse the caseload, route history, diagnosis or another NGO's cases. A funder receives approved project requests and sufficiently aggregated outcomes; there is no patient directory. Public access, if ever added, covers generic service information only.

Enforce role plus organisation, geography, assignment and purpose on the server; hiding a UI tab is insufficient. Demo accounts and fixtures are labelled. Record access/revocation and state transitions; provide a retention/deletion policy, safe-contact preference and grievance route before a pilot. Do not require a household smartphone, OTP, Aadhaar scan or public story for assistance. A provider badge states the exact identity/readiness/catchment checks and dates, rather than “government verified.” Household health permission does not automatically authorize NGO disclosure or donor reporting.

Do not claim that CSV import is connected to RCH, U-WIN, Shaishav, ABDM or Darpan. Public standards and source-code availability are not record-access permission. ABDM can represent service requests, so the design also must not claim it is technically incapable of referrals. Production interoperability needs authorised agreements, tested mappings and security review. [Official ABDM consent/sandbox/security description](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2152537&lang=2&reg=48), [published FHIR guide](https://www.nrces.in/ndhm/fhir/r4/index.html).

### Funding is a record of decisions, without custody

The selected navigation demo uses synthetic in-kind appointment capacity at no beneficiary cost. Show a compact optional funding progression: `not_required` or `request_draft -> cost_reviewed -> approval_pending -> approved/declined -> external_disbursement_reported or in_kind_supplied -> receipt_recorded -> reconciled`. Approval has a named authorised NGO/funder owner; an app administrator does not invent that authority. If funding is necessary to provide the service, acceptance must be conditional until approval or a provider commitment to supply it without that funding is recorded.

These states neither transfer money nor certify financial compliance. No donations, custody, wallet, fund balance, escrow, “available CSR pool” or guaranteed grant is part of the 24-hour build. External receipt status is reported metadata with appropriate review, not payment-provider confirmation. Before actual money movement, partners need source/entity-specific financial and legal review. CSR Exchange itself distinguishes platform/project interaction from funding decisions. [CSR Exchange FAQ](https://www.csrxchange.gov.in/Frontend/faq).

Use assertions on state transitions, row counts, capacity, access and feasibility, not screenshots alone. These cover material safety and shared-state behavior; avoid spending the hackathon on tests that merely restate labels.

### Metrics that do not manufacture impact

Compare against a reproducible baseline using the **same validated tasks, assignment, priorities, shift, availability and travel model**: priority/due-ordered task selection with append-only route construction in stable task-ID order inside ties. Show an optional simple distance-first route as an explicit tradeoff demonstration, with its priority misses exposed. Save the seed, inputs, algorithm versions and denominators. Do not compare an infeasible unrestricted baseline to a feasible constrained route without prominently reporting that difference.

Report estimated travel minutes, total planned minutes, visits served by bucket, due coverage, remote/locality coverage, overdue/deferral counts and the reasons for exclusions. A travel percentage is `(baseline - proposed) / baseline` only when baseline is nonzero and comparable; report no improvement or increased travel when that is the result. Display **simulation estimate on synthetic data**. Do not promise 30% savings, lives saved, clinically safer classification, optimality or field-proven outcomes.

For referrals, show matching, offered, accepted, reported delivered, confirmed and unresolved counts separately. Demo wall-clock response times are scripted behavior, not service-level evidence. A pilot would measure operator response, time to accepted appointment, confirmed session completion, unresolved barrier aging, user burden and unserved fairness, with observation windows and exclusions stated. Programme benefit approval and health outcomes need their own evidence.

Before a pilot, confirm what tools the catchment already uses and whether configuration or an add-on would serve it better. Obtain the actual 2026 programme rules and priority approval; a willing health team, coordinator and service providers; funded coordination and verification time; beneficiary/worker usability review; secure hosting, access auditing, tested backup/restore and incident handling; approved record-sharing and retention arrangements; a field-tested travel model; transaction/conflict tests; and realistic provider capacity/expiry/complaint procedures. Partner operations and physical service availability are prerequisites, not things software creates. Privacy implementation and legal review must use the applicable notified timeline rather than claiming that all DPDP provisions were fully effective on the report date. [Official staged commencement notification](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf).

### Minimal winning demonstration

In four minutes: import the synthetic CSV; show a remote approved-priority visit preserved in a feasible day and one missed case with a reason; compare estimated travel and bucket coverage honestly; complete a visit and record a document-access barrier; show an out-of-catchment/stale provider rejected; obtain recipient-specific permission and have the eligible provider accept one current slot; record help delivered then independently confirmed; finish on the coordinator view showing the still-unresolved entitlement step. A brief replay of two competing acceptance events for the last slot demonstrates technical credibility. The story succeeds by showing one usable day and one accountable support handoff, with failure visible.
