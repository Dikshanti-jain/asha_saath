# Eight maternal, child and health programs for ASHA–NGO coordination

Research cutoff: **8 October 2026, India**. This module contains exactly eight distinct program entries. Official sources were checked with connected Tavily search/extraction and direct web access; the Tavily deep-research request failed with HTTP 504, so findings were verified against the primary sources below. A website footer update is not treated as the date of a new scheme rule. Benefits require the administering authority's verification; local facilities, implementation orders and beneficiary status must be checked before a referral.

## 1. Janani Suraksha Yojana — JSY

**Purpose and eligibility.** JSY promotes institutional delivery to reduce maternal/neonatal mortality. NHM's current page distinguishes low performing states (LPS) from high performing states (HPS): all women delivering in government facilities in LPS; BPL/SC/ST women in HPS government facilities; BPL/SC/ST women in accredited private facilities in either group. The same page lists mother's cash packages as LPS ₹1,400 rural/₹1,000 urban and HPS ₹700 rural/₹600 urban. ASHA packages are **₹600 rural and ₹400 urban**, separate from the mother's benefit; the national page splits them into ANC and delivery-facilitation components. Do not advertise a combined mother-plus-ASHA figure as the mother's payment. Check the state's current classification and instructions, particularly where the national page retains legacy geography. [NHM JSY page](https://nhm.gov.in/nhm/index1.php?lang=1&level=3&lid=309&sublinkid=841) (publication date unstated; site footer 7 Oct 2026; accessed 8 Oct 2026).

**Where/how to refer.** National centrally sponsored scheme under NHM, implemented by states/districts. Identify the woman's ASHA/ANM and a delivery-capable government subcentre, PHC, CHC, first-referral unit or district/state hospital, or a currently accredited private institution. Obtain the facility's own eligibility/document checklist, record the submission acknowledgment, and separately track whether the mother's payment was received. Facility accreditation is a referral check, not an assumption based on private ownership.

**ASHA and NGO complement.** ASHA links the woman to ANC, delivery and post-delivery care. An NGO can help with language access, document preparation, travel planning, appointment reminders and payment-status escalation, with the woman's consent. An NGO's support should strengthen the ASHA/ANM/facility workflow, not make eligibility decisions or collect the woman's benefit.

**Bottleneck evidence.** CAG's Haryana Report No. 2 of 2024 documented delayed/non-payment and missing records in tested facilities for earlier periods, including 2016–21. This establishes a historical, location-specific payment-tracking problem; it does **not** establish the October 2026 delay rate in a new district. Missing banking/documents or late submission are operational risks to test locally, not claimed national prevalence. [CAG Haryana public-health audit](https://cag.gov.in/webroot/uploads/download_audit_report/2024/English_Final_Haryana-Report-No2-06734a09867e211.27299110.pdf) (2024, chapter 7).

**NGO funding status.** JSY payments are beneficiary/ASHA entitlements under applicable rules. No automatic NGO grant or outreach contract was verified. Discuss a costed support proposal with the District Health Society/state NHM only through a documented procurement or partnership route.

## 2. Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram — JSSK

**Purpose and benefit.** JSSK addresses out-of-pocket costs of public-facility delivery and sick-infant treatment. The NHM page confirms free delivery including C-section, drugs/consumables, diagnostics, blood when required, maternal diet for three days after normal delivery and seven after C-section, and home-to-facility/interfacility/drop-back transport. Entitlements extend to antenatal/postnatal pregnancy complications and sick newborns/infants **up to one year** in public institutions. These are service entitlements, not a cash transfer or a promise of equivalent reimbursement at any private hospital. [NHM JSSK page](https://nhm.gov.in/nhm_live/index1.php?lang=1&level=3&lid=308&sublinkid=842) (launch June 2011; extension recorded 2014; site footer 6 Oct 2026; accessed 8 Oct 2026).

**Coverage and referral.** National public-health initiative, operationalized by states through rural and urban public facilities and transport/referral systems. Map the nearest delivery point, C-section/referral hospital and facility able to treat a sick infant. Ask the ANM or facility to confirm transport booking, referral contact, diagnostics and required supplies; ASHA can explain entitlements and accompany/facilitate an appropriate referral within local duties. A family should not wait for a benefit application before seeking urgent clinical care.

**NGO complement.** Provide a local-language entitlement card, help families contact the public transport/facility team, support accessible navigation, and document charges or denial for beneficiary-led escalation. If an NGO offers backup travel or accompaniment from its own funds, clearly label it as NGO support and record the reason; it is not evidence that JSSK's public transport entitlement has disappeared.

**Bottleneck evidence.** The NHM page records high delivery-related expenses as the scheme's original rationale. Its historical background percentages must not be presented as current prevalence. Transport non-arrival, supply gaps and inappropriate charging are **local operational risks** requiring district service records or a current audit before being described as demonstrated failures.

**Funding.** No automatic NGO grant was verified. Referral-transport or other service contracts, where invited by a state, require their own tender, authorization and service standards; do not infer a contract merely because JSSK provides transport.

## 3. Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana — PMMVY

**Purpose, amounts and milestones.** PMMVY partially compensates maternity wage loss and promotes health-seeking. WCD's official FAQ specifies first-child ₹5,000 as ₹3,000 after pregnancy registration and at least two ANC checks, preferably within six months of LMP, plus ₹2,000 after registered birth and completion of the first immunization cycle at fourteen weeks. For a **second child who is a girl**, ₹6,000 is paid in one instalment after the corresponding pregnancy, birth and immunization conditions. JSY can also apply, subject to its own rules; do not promise a fixed combined total. Citizen application and ASHA/AWW-assisted online application are supported. [WCD PMMVY page](https://www.wcd.gov.in/women/pradhan-mantri-matru-vandana-yojna) (date unstated; accessed 8 Oct 2026).

**Eligibility caveats.** Accepted categories include SC/ST, disability of at least 40%, BPL/NFSA ration card, PM-JAY, e-Shram, MGNREGA job card, PM-Kisan beneficiary, family income below ₹8 lakh per year, and pregnant/lactating AWW/AWH/ASHA, with prescribed proof. This is not universal unconditional payment to every pregnancy. The 18 July 2023 order adds NFSA ration-card eligibility and clarifies income-proof acceptance. The 17 April 2023 order specifies age **18 years seven months–55**; the FAQ describes eligibility at childbirth as under 55. The 20 February 2023 order sets an application window of **270 days after childbirth**. Check portal application of the endpoint and any subsequent state instruction. [Eligibility order](https://wcd.gov.in/images/uploaded/1710112241_amP5P6rxQB.pdf), [age order](https://wcd.gov.in/images/uploaded/1710112241_5DWkILT5au.pdf), [time-limit order](https://wcd.gov.in/images/uploaded/1710112241_fm5Lgec9R2.pdf).

Mission Shakti guidelines exclude regular Central/State Government or PSU employees and women receiving similar maternity benefits under another law. For second-girl-child benefits, registration **during pregnancy is mandatory**; do not interpret the later application window as permission to omit pregnancy registration. The current FAQ allows fresh-beneficiary treatment after miscarriage/stillbirth in a future pregnancy and removes mandatory husband's Aadhaar. [Mission Shakti implementation guidelines](https://wcd.gov.in/documents/uploaded/1714563459_tx4qgk0riq.pdf) (2022 framework; accessed 8 Oct 2026).

**Where and current geography.** Use the nearest AWC/ASHA, state-approved health facility and [PMMVY citizen portal](https://pmmvy.wcd.gov.in). A **12 August 2026** parliamentary reply says implementation is in **35 States/UTs including Odisha**, which began FY 2025–26; **Telangana was still being onboarded**. This newer source supersedes the FAQ's older statement excluding both Odisha and Telangana. Confirm current Telangana onboarding before routing applications. [PIB/WCD update](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2298273&reg=3&lang=1).

**ASHA/NGO workflow.** With consent, check one accepted eligibility proof, beneficiary Aadhaar, Aadhaar-mapped bank/post-office account, mobile number, MCP/RCH records, ANC, birth registration and vaccine records. ASHA/AWW can submit and track within authorized jurisdiction. An NGO can assist the woman to obtain corrections from the actual bank/civil-registration/health authority, preserve acknowledgment and refer unresolved cases to the local administering officer. [PMMVY 2.0 FAQ](https://wcd.gov.in/images/uploaded/1710098119_HDEXdpWMb1.pdf) (undated document; accessed 8 Oct 2026).

**Bottleneck and funding.** The official FAQ evidences documentary/payment prerequisites; whether Aadhaar-bank mismatch, missing birth records or connectivity causes delays locally is a **hypothesis to audit**, not a verified nationwide rate. The ministry announced **1515** for POSHAN/PMMVY from 1 Nov 2025; confirm local connection. Benefit money is not NGO income; ASHA/AWW honorarium rules do not create NGO entitlement. [Helpline announcement, 24 Oct 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2182173&reg=48&lang=2).

## 4. Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan — PMSMA

**Purpose and eligibility.** PMSMA offers free quality ANC for pregnant women in the **second or third trimester** at designated government facilities on the **ninth of each month**. The official portal describes diagnostics/counselling, detection and line-listing of high-risk pregnancy, management of conditions and birth/complication planning, with specialist/physician review. Confirm the locally scheduled session; an emergency must use the immediate clinical referral route. [Official PMSMA portal](https://pmsma.mohfw.gov.in/?lang=en) (program introduced 2016; portal carries September 2026 activity; accessed 8 Oct 2026).

**Geography and pathway.** National program under MoHFW; states and districts identify participating rural/urban government sites and referral facilities. An NGO should obtain the local facility roster, session timetable and named high-risk referral contact from the district maternal-health team. Routine ANC remains necessary between program visits.

**ASHA and NGO complement.** Official guidelines assign ASHA awareness, beneficiary line-listing and mobilization; ANM/facility staff provide ANC and organize clinical referral. NGOs can support appointment reminders, interpretation, transport/navigation and tracking whether the referred woman reached the treating facility. Qualified obstetricians/radiologists/physicians may explore the official volunteer registration channel; NGO affiliation alone does not authorize clinical practice. [PMSMA operational guidelines](https://pmsma.mohfw.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Pradhan_Mantri_Surakshit_Matritva_Abhiyan.pdf) (2016 document; sections on ASHA/ANM roles).

**Bottleneck evidence.** Missed ANC and high-risk pregnancy identification are explicit program objectives. A specialist being absent, diagnostics being unavailable or a high-risk referral failing to close are **site-specific risks** to verify from attendance, test and referral records; the reviewed sources do not prove their current district prevalence.

**Funding.** The volunteering channel is a service opportunity, not an NGO grant or salary commitment. Any funded outreach, transport or clinical arrangement requires its own state/district authorization and terms.

## 5. Surakshit Matritva Aashwasan — SUMAN

**Purpose and entitlement boundary.** SUMAN aims for dignified, respectful and quality maternal/newborn care at no cost, with zero tolerance for denial in public facilities. Its service charter includes at least four ANC checks, home-based newborn-care visits, trained delivery, complication management, breastfeeding support, transport/referral, newborn vaccination and grievance redressal. It integrates existing initiatives including JSSK and PMSMA. It is **not a separately additive cash benefit**; the charter refers beneficiaries to conditional transfers under the applicable schemes. [Official SUMAN portal and charter](https://www.suman.mohfw.gov.in/) (page last updated 10 July 2021; accessed through official indexed content 8 Oct 2026).

**Geography and pathway.** National assurance initiative implemented through states' public maternity/newborn networks. Ask the district for its current designated SUMAN facilities and referral map, and confirm which level handles normal delivery, obstetric complications and sick newborn care. The older portal's reference to referral within an hour is a service-guarantee aspiration; do not promise an actual travel time for every village.

**ASHA and NGO complement.** ASHA can explain entitlements, help birth planning and link facility care to authorized home follow-up. NGOs can provide language/accessibility support, birth-companion navigation where facility policy permits, beneficiary feedback and grievance assistance. Record the woman's account of a service denial with consent, retain referral/receipt details, and help her approach the facility help desk and district maternal-health officer. The portal lists a grievance number **1800-180-1104** and web registration; because its page is dated 2021, validate the working channel before printing it locally.

**Bottleneck evidence.** A zero-denial charter and complaint mechanism establish standards; they do not establish how frequently denial occurs in 2026. Poor respectful-care experiences, missed home visits and inaccessible complaints are **implementation hypotheses** until supported by a current facility audit or beneficiary feedback sample.

**Funding.** No automatic NGO grant was verified. Community engagement can be proposed to state/district NHM through a written partnership, approved outreach plan or procurement; any NGO clinical service requires distinct permissions.

## 6. Mission Indradhanush — MI, including intensified rounds

**Purpose and beneficiaries.** MI targets missed and partially vaccinated children and pregnant women, particularly underserved/high-risk settlements, through intensified routine-immunization outreach. **IMI 5.0 was a dated 2023 campaign**, conducted in all districts, including children up to five years and pregnant women; previous rounds commonly used younger child age bands. It delivered vaccines already in UIP according to the National Immunization Schedule. Do not describe IMI 5.0's August–October 2023 sessions as an open October 2026 campaign. [MoHFW/PIB IMI 5.0 release, 12 Oct 2023](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1966931&lang=2&reg=3).

**Geography and access.** National strategy implemented through state/district microplans and notified rounds; rural hamlets, remote habitations, urban slums and migratory/temporary settlements are practical outreach targets. Ask the District Immunization Officer/ANM whether a current catch-up round is notified and where sessions will operate. If no campaign session is current, route due vaccines to the continuing UIP service.

**ASHA and NGO complement.** Under ANM/district microplanning, ASHA/AWW/community mobilizers identify due beneficiaries and bring families to sessions. NGO staff can help find missed mobile families, translate approved information, address travel/time barriers and support reminders, while trained authorized vaccinators administer and document vaccines. Vaccine hesitancy or access barriers should be discussed respectfully; do not claim an NGO can create independent government immunization records or access U-WIN through its own system without approval.

**Bottleneck evidence.** The official 2023 release identifies missed/dropped-out beneficiaries as the campaign's target. Local reasons—migration, session timing, misinformation, transport or stock availability—must be measured rather than assumed.

**Overlap and accounting.** MI is a campaign mechanism **within the UIP immunization ecosystem**, not a second vaccine entitlement. Use one child/pregnancy record and one dose event. Tag the event's delivery setting as MI/catch-up if relevant; do not sum MI participants with UIP participants to estimate unique reach, and do not count each return dose as a new beneficiary.

**Funding.** No automatic NGO grant was verified. Support requires a district-approved campaign role, a formal implementation arrangement where available, or the NGO's own funding.

## 7. Universal Immunization Programme — UIP

**Purpose and benefit.** UIP is the continuing national routine program providing free scheduled vaccination for children/adolescents and pregnant women. MoHFW's 2024–25 annual report supplies an updated national schedule including newborn and infant doses, later boosters, Td at ages ten/sixteen and pregnancy Td, with Japanese encephalitis vaccination in endemic districts. Clinical staff must determine due/catch-up doses from the current schedule and past records. Do not use an old web PDF showing phased vaccine introduction as proof of October 2026 availability. [MoHFW Annual Report 2024–25, immunization chapter and schedule, p. 72](https://mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/Final%20Printed%20English%20AR%202024-25.pdf).

**Geography and service point.** National program, delivered by state/district immunization teams through fixed public-facility and outreach sessions in both rural and urban areas. Local access includes village/AWC/subcentre sessions, PHCs/UPHCs and hospitals. Maharashtra's state NHM page explicitly describes free institution/outreach vaccination and illustrates state-specific endemic-district arrangements; its details must not be copied as another state's service map. [Maharashtra NHM routine immunisation page, 17 Apr 2025](https://nhm.maharashtra.gov.in/en/scheme/routine-immunisation-programme/).

**ASHA and NGO complement.** With ANM/AWW, maintain a due list, explain the next visit, remind caregivers and record referral completion against official vaccination documentation. NGO assistance is useful for temporary settlements, migrants, accessible information and continuity across relocation. Vaccination and adverse-event assessment belong to trained authorized health personnel; NGOs can assist navigation back to the health team.

**Bottleneck evidence.** The official health-worker handbook explicitly requires microplanning that includes hamlets, urban subwards, migratory populations and temporary settlements, and tracking dropouts. It demonstrates recognized operational needs, not the local share of missed children. Validate local due lists, session cancellations and coverage before selecting an intervention. [NHM Immunization Handbook for Health Workers, 2018](https://www.nhm.gov.in/New_Updates_2018/NHM_Components/Immunization/Guildelines_for_immunization/Immunization_Handbook_for_Health_Workers-English.pdf) (use here only for planning roles; clinical schedules require newer guidance).

**Overlap/funding.** A dose delivered during MI remains a UIP dose and is counted once. No automatic NGO grant, vaccine-administration authorization or government-data integration entitlement was verified.

## 8. Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana — AB PM-JAY

**Purpose and core benefit.** PM-JAY protects eligible families against large covered hospital expenses: **up to ₹5 lakh per family per year** for secondary/tertiary hospitalization at empanelled public/private hospitals, cashless at the point of service, including specified pre/post-hospitalization expenses. Pre-existing conditions are covered from day one; family size/age/gender limits do not restrict members of an eligible family. This is not ₹5 lakh cash paid to each patient, and routine outpatient care or every private-hospital service is not automatically covered. [MoHFW RML Hospital official key-features page](https://rmlh.mohfw.gov.in/index1.aspx?langid=1&lev=2&lid=3620&lsid=2567) (last updated 5 Feb 2025).

**Eligibility and 70+ expansion.** Verify the household in the official beneficiary system and applicable state-added categories rather than treating poverty, ASHA referral or an ABHA number as sufficient eligibility proof. The 11 September 2024 Cabinet approval expanded eligibility to Indian senior citizens **70+ irrespective of income**. Already-covered families receive an additional up-to-₹5-lakh senior-only annual top-up, not a general ₹10-lakh entitlement for younger members. For other senior families, ₹5 lakh is shared on a family basis. Seniors with CGHS/ECHS/CAPF benefits must choose the applicable public scheme; the official clarification allows seniors with private insurance/ESI to use this expansion. [Cabinet approval, 11 Sep 2024](https://nha.gov.in/img/resources/Press_Release.pdf), [NHA senior-citizen FAQ](https://nha.gov.in/img/resources/English_FAQs_related_to_the_benefits_for_senior_citizens.pdf) (FAQ date unstated; accessed 8 Oct 2026).

**Geography and referral.** NHA sets the national framework, State Health Agencies operate state arrangements, and care is delivered by currently empanelled hospitals with applicable specialties/packages and authorization. **Delhi became the 35th implementing State/UT on 5 April 2025**; older sources excluding Delhi and Odisha are stale. This dated count is not a guarantee of an accessible hospital in every district. Verify participation, hospital empanelment, relevant package and portability before travel. [PIB Delhi implementation update, 5 Apr 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2119281).

**ASHA and NGO complement.** ASHA can help households learn about coverage and obtain a verified referral; NGOs can assist official card/eligibility checks, language access, transport planning and handoff to the hospital's Ayushman help desk. Clinical admission/authorization is decided through the hospital/SHA workflow. The official April 2025 release also reports inclusion of frontline ASHA/Anganwadi workers in health coverage; verify the worker/family record rather than assuming an ID alone activates it.

**Bottleneck/funding.** Incomplete identity verification, a distant suitable empanelled hospital and authorization/document gaps are **operational hypotheses** here, not claimed October 2026 national failure rates. Count completed covered admissions separately from card creation. No NGO grant was verified. A charitable hospital can explore provider empanelment only if it meets current requirements; reimbursement for authorized care is provider payment, not general NGO fundraising.

## Evidence notes and district implementation checklist

1. **Current state exception verified:** PMMVY's August 2026 PIB reply takes precedence over the older FAQ's Odisha exclusion. Telangana onboarding should be rechecked at use. The February 2023 time-limit order describes one ANC check; the currently linked WCD FAQ describes at least two. This report uses the current FAQ for milestone preparation and flags the difference for the administering officer/portal rather than promising approval under the older wording. Scheme benefits and application rules still require local validation.
2. **Do not turn old totals into market size:** No historical launch estimate, cumulative campaign total, visits, card count or hospital admissions is used here as the number of unique October 2026 eligible people. Portal activity totals were deliberately omitted.
3. **Program overlap:** One pregnancy may use JSY, PMMVY, JSSK, PMSMA and SUMAN. Store each scheme's status separately but deduplicate the woman/pregnancy. UIP and MI must share one child/dose history; campaign tags describe how a dose was delivered.
4. **Evidence labels:** Historical CAG findings establish a documented past/local bottleneck; charter standards and documentary requirements establish expected process. Unmeasured local obstacles are labeled hypotheses, not national findings.
5. **Practical first contact:** District Health Society/CMHO or equivalent for NHM maternal services; District Immunization Officer for UIP/MI; state-selected WCD/social-welfare/health PMMVY officer and AWC/ASHA for PMMVY; State Health Agency and empanelled-hospital help desk for PM-JAY. Titles vary by state.
6. **Minimum field directory:** Facility name and geography, service capability, designated session, clinical/administrative contact, referral destination, transport route, applicable eligibility/document checklist, escalation channel, last verified date, and a responsible staff member. A national page cannot replace this district directory.
7. **NGO proposal route:** Agree a narrow problem and outcome—completed high-risk referral, due-dose completion, acknowledged maternity application or resolved denial—with the competent authority. Obtain a written role, scope, permissions, escalation contacts and data-sharing terms. Funding must come from a documented grant/contract/CSR/own-resource arrangement; none of these eight program names itself creates NGO funding eligibility.
8. **Data and beneficiary rights:** Use informed consent, minimal access and authorized records. NGOs should not ask for staff credentials or promise direct integration with government beneficiary systems. Provide assistance without representing the NGO as the sanctioning or clinical authority.

All URLs above are primary Indian government sources. Source dates are given where published; otherwise the date is explicitly recorded as unstated and access was 8 October 2026. Local service performance and approved NGO agreements remain to be verified district by district.
