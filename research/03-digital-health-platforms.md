# Eight India-facing digital health platforms: ASHA and NGO referral relevance

Research cut-off: **8 October 2026**. Exactly eight platforms are counted: ANMOL, RCH Portal, U-WIN, AMRIT, CommCare, eSanjeevani, ABDM ecosystem and Ni-kshay. AMRIT's FLW app and its API are one platform; ABDM's ABHA/HFR/HPR/HIE-CM building blocks are one ecosystem. ANMOL and RCH are distinct applications in the same government programme, with substantial integration and overlap, rather than independent competitors.

**Finding:** ASHA worklists, offline collection, counseling media, task prioritization and clinical referrals already exist in this landscape. A defensible opportunity is an additional, locally operated service-fulfillment layer: verify an NGO's actual service and coverage, obtain consent, identify an eligible beneficiary, secure acceptance from an available provider, complete the service, return the outcome and escalate failure. This is a hypothesis requiring district validation, not a claim that all eight platforms fail or cannot be configured to do it. Ni-kshay provides a particularly important existing counterexample: its TB community-support programme explicitly connects consented patients, district officers and donors/NGOs and includes follow-up confirmation.

Evidence labels: **Official** = government/operator technical documentation; **Provider claim** = product description, not an independently verified performance result; **Field evidence** = a primary evaluation in the named setting; **Not demonstrated** = not established by the sources reviewed, not proof of absence. Dates below are source dates or the explicit date attached to a statistic; search-engine crawl/publication estimates were not treated as document dates.

## Where a national-to-local service network could break

The following are **failure hypotheses to test**, except where an explicit documented dependency is identified. They should not be reported as proven nationwide failures of the platforms.

| Handoff | Potential break and why it matters | Concrete validation / design requirement |
|---|---|---|
| National NGO identity → district service listing | Legal identity, a health facility registration or a donor commitment does not establish that a particular district office supplies a specific service today. | Verify district contact, service eligibility, geographic coverage, staff qualifications where relevant, delivery mode, charges and a renewal date. Keep organisation verification separate from service availability. |
| ASHA due task → appropriate referral | A clinical due-list or consultation can identify need without naming an accessible nonclinical provider. An app may already support referral forms, but the recipient still needs to be connected operationally. | Test the actual beneficiary journey for transport, food assistance, disability support or other chosen services; identify who can refer, what data they see and who is responsible for follow-up. |
| Directory match → accepted slot | A listing can be accurate while the NGO is full, outside catchment, unable to fund the service or unwilling to accept a case. | Require explicit acceptance/decline, current capacity, response deadline, queue ownership and an alternative provider/escalation. Measure unanswered referrals, not just referral counts. |
| Consent → appropriate data sharing | ABDM consented health-record exchange and Ni-kshay's support consent are documented, but purpose, roles and data minimization differ for social support. | Record service-specific consent; share only necessary referral data with the accepting NGO; maintain an assisted route and withdrawal handling. Reuse existing official exchange only with authorized access. |
| Offline task → consistent shared state | ANMOL/AMRIT/CommCare/U-WIN already support offline use. Offline functionality does not ensure that a second provider sees newly accepted/completed tasks before synchronization. | Test sync latency, duplicate identities, conflicting updates and failed delivery under real district connectivity. Do not advertise offline as a unique new feature. |
| Accepted referral → completed service | Consultation, reported dose, recorded support count and actual beneficiary experience are different outcomes. Ni-kshay already prescribes follow-up confirmation, showing the importance of this step. | Record accepted, scheduled, attempted, delivered, beneficiary-confirmed, declined and failed states; use proportionate evidence, independent sample checks and a complaint route. |
| Completion → ASHA/supervisor accountability | App dashboards can track activities while unresolved cross-organisation referrals have no clear owner. Historical CommCare field evidence shows supervision did not automatically improve with digital tools. | Assign a named case owner and supervisor escalation, return concise outcomes to the ASHA, and monitor unresolved time and beneficiary-reported delivery alongside entries. |
| National scale → sustainable district operation | Software, state deployment, training and physical service capacity must all be funded. Existing pilots/implementation documents do not prove uniform scale or lasting capacity. | Pilot a few services in two contrasting districts; cost case coordination and field verification; obtain district/state workflow ownership; document minimum staffing and financing before scale. |

## Evidence cautions and exact source notes

1. **ANMOL features vs current state.** The [28 May 2024 manual](https://nhmmizoram.org/upload/Anmol%205.0.12%20User%20Manual_28May2024.pdf), pp.5–8, 53–59 and 97, is operational documentation, not a 2026 impact evaluation. The [MoHFW Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_US&id=org.unicef.eanmapp) names the operator and was updated 18 Mar 2025 in the fetched listing; it mentions immunization synchronization fixes. User reviews describe particular difficulties but are not representative evidence and were not used to declare nationwide failure. Offline counseling media should not be confused with voice data entry.
2. **RCH current deployment.** The [MoHFW annual report 2025–26](https://notto.mohfw.gov.in/WriteReadData/Portal/News/951_1_Annual_Report_of_the_MoH_FW__2025_-_2026.pdf), p.50, is the dated source for RCH and ANMOL deployment and cumulative records. It describes RCH 2.0 as an upgrade and should not be used to claim universal implementation of future features. The state [Maharashtra workflow](https://nhm.maharashtra.gov.in/en/scheme/reproductive-child-health-rch-portal/) was undated in the reviewed text; its printout/data-entry route is specific documentation, not a measured current performance result for all states.
3. **U-WIN chronology.** [PIB 29 Nov 2024](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2079025&lang=2&reg=3) establishes completed nationwide rollout and features. [Rajya Sabha 10 Mar 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/270/AU2046_mmlLm6.pdf?source=pqars) verifies present integration and offline recording. Registration totals used here have an explicit 18 Mar 2026 date; no extrapolation to 8 Oct 2026 was made. SMS reminders require a reachable recorded phone; their availability is not evidence of universal receipt or action.
4. **AMRIT marketing vs code vs impact.** [Piramal product page](https://amrit.piramalswasthya.org/products/front-line-worker-app/) is an undated operator claim for speech entry and workplans; [FLW repository](https://github.com/PSMRI/FLW-Mobile-App) documents modules and offline/multilingual design. [API repository](https://github.com/PSMRI/FLW-API) establishes published REST service code. These do not independently measure voice accuracy, sync reliability, referral completion or state-wide active use. Named Bihar/Assam variants must not become a claim of nationwide ASHA coverage.
5. **CommCare prioritization is established.** [September 2021 Precision Tasking article](https://commcare.dimagi.com/blog/precision-tasking-framework/) remains available at the current CommCare domain and linked from the [current India services page](https://dimagi.com/services/india/). It is a customizable framework/reference application, not evidence that every deployment contains the framework. The older [API Authentication page, updated 19 Apr 2024](https://dimagi.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/commcarepublic/pages/2143946031) is explicitly **archived** and points to API Overview; it should not be used to describe current SSO rules. The [March 2026 announcement](https://forum.dimagi.com/t/action-required-api-authentication-change-for-sso-enabled-projects/12235) supplies the newer API-key requirement.
6. **Primary CommCare field evidence and limitation.** [Bihar trial, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6875677/) used baseline 2012 and follow-up 2014 in Saharsa. It reports improved ASHA/AWW coordination and certain home visits/behaviours; FLW supervision was not significantly affected. The [Mathematica issue brief, 20 Oct 2015](https://www.mathematica.org/publications/a-mobile-tool-for-health-workers-promising-results-in-bihar-india) notes technical/logistical challenges and underused features despite positive effects. These are historical setting-specific findings, not proof that the current platform universally fails. No current RCT of cross-NGO India referral fulfillment was identified.
7. **eSanjeevani source interpretation.** [C-DAC product page](https://www.cdac.gov.in/index.aspx?id=product_details&productId=eSanjeevaniNationalTelemedicineService) supplies the explicit 16 Aug 2026 network/consultation counts. The [2026 government response](https://abdm.gov.in/strapicms/uploads/AU_4663_kpy_VQG_c47376a51b.pdf) supplies all-State/UT rollout, frontline facilitation and state/NHM implementation. Service volumes establish real scale but do not measure continuity of social support or NGO referral closure. No statewide absence of specialist capacity was inferred.
8. **ABDM standards are broader than identity.** [Official HFR release, 6 Jul 2022](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1839609&lang=2&reg=3) covers public/private health facilities and interoperable APIs. [Official 5 Aug 2025 release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2152537&lang=2&reg=48) covers security audit/sandbox/consent. [Published FHIR v6.5.0](https://www.nrces.in/ndhm/fhir/r4/index.html) includes ServiceRequest, Appointment, CarePlan and Organisation; therefore describing ABDM as only a health ID or unable to carry referral information is inaccurate. The [v7 preview](https://www.nrces.in/preview/ndhm/fhir/r4/index.html) labels itself a local development build with draft date 15 Jul 2026. Official sandbox portal returned 403 to this research browser; this access failure was not treated as proof that APIs are unavailable to approved integrators.
9. **Ni-kshay already has service support closure.** [CTD 2022 guidance](https://tbcindia.mohfw.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/1583929709Guidance-Booklet_02-08-2022.pdf), pp.7–17, describes patient consent, donor/NGO registration, district needs assessment, agreed delivery and DTO follow-up confirmation. Its instructions include involving NGOs/SHGs/supply chains and restrictions on repurposing patient data. This is a designed operational pathway, not independent evidence of completion for every registered commitment. The [family caregiver guide, March 2023 file](https://tbcindia.mohfw.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/1108732099Family-Care-Model-Guidebook_Revised_27th-Mar_Print-ready-1.pdf), p.33, confirms caregiver tagging examples; neither donor registration nor a caregiver role establishes broad NGO accreditation.
