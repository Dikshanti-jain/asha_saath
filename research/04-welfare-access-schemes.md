# Welfare and care access: eight reserved programmes

Research cut-off: **8 October 2026, India**. This note counts exactly eight catalogue entries. NSAP is counted once, NMHP includes Tele-MANAS, and NTEP includes its nutrition-payment component. PM TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan/Ni-kshay Mitra is a linked community-support initiative rather than an independent TB treatment system. Software portals named below are access/infrastructure channels, not additional schemes.

All sources below are official government or government-implementing-agency sources. Publication/effective dates are distinguished from website-update dates. National programme design does not establish that every facility, camp or NGO partner is available in a particular village.

**NSAP amounts and eligibility nuance.** The 1 January 2026 MoRD table gives central old-age pension assistance of ₹200/month at 60–79 and ₹500 at 80+; widow pension ₹300 at 40–79 and ₹500 at 80+; disability pension ₹300 at 18–79 for BPL people with 80% disability and ₹500 at 80+. These are **central contributions**, not uniform total pensions. State pension programmes/top-ups can have different criteria. The umbrella also contains non-pension support, which is not separately counted here. [MoRD year-end review, 1 Jan 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2210378&lang=1&reg=3)

The 2014 guidelines say an otherwise eligible person absent from the BPL list should have eligibility established rather than simply be excluded. They require application and local verification, with proactive household identification. This is not evidence that all low-income residents automatically qualify. The 17 March 2026 reply documents State/UT beneficiary ceilings; this is a structural limit on central funding, not proof that every locality has a waiting list. [NSAP guidelines](https://rural.gov.in/sites/default/files/NSAP_Guidelines_English.pdf), [2026 funding reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2241352&lang=1&reg=1)

**ADIP verification warning.** The authoritative September 2024 PDF provides full/50% assistance tiers at income up to ₹22,500/₹22,501–30,000 monthly, subject to device-cost ceilings. General same-purpose repeat support is after three years; for under-18s, customized items have a one-year interval, with named five-year exceptions. ADIP-SSA has special certification pathways, including some children below 40% disability, so the general threshold must not be applied mechanically to every child. Assessment, prescription, fitment and user training require qualified rehabilitation professionals. [26 Sep 2024 guidelines, sections 6–8](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3e58aea67b01fa747687f038dfde066f6/uploads/2024/09/202409271355861874.pdf)

The [DEPwD scheme page](https://depwd.gov.in/en/adip/) still states continuation approval only through **31 March 2026**. The live [e-Anudaan portal](https://grants.depwd.gov.in/ngo-login), checked 8 October 2026, invites ADIP proposals for **FY2026–27**. This establishes ongoing operational activity, but a fresh continuation order was not located. Confirm applicable funding/current limits with the implementing agency before promising an award. The ARJUN-hosted ADIP PDF returned an April 2024 document despite a newer search label; use the department-linked September PDF for repeat-assistance rules.

**RVY documentation and logistics.** ALIMCO’s undated FAQ gives Aadhaar plus BPL/pension or accepted income evidence and lists mobility aids, hearing aids, spectacles and other assessed devices. RVY does not require treating an age-related infirmity as an ADIP-certified 40% disability. The 2025–26 annual report confirms RVY remains an ALIMCO-delivered AVYAY component with camp delivery. For routing, verify assessment date, device suitability, delivery and repair contact rather than assuming the next camp can supply the requested item. [ALIMCO FAQ](https://alimco.in/WriteReadData/CMS/Awareness.pdf), [DSJE annual report](https://socialjustice.gov.in/writereaddata/UploadFile/71441776233188.pdf)

**RVY continuation evidence gap:** this pass did not verify a fresh RVY/AVYAY continuation order for the period after 31 March 2026. The live [ARJUN registration portal](https://adip.depwd.gov.in/) still includes RVY, but portal availability alone does not establish formal renewal or a funded local camp. Confirm the current authorization and local availability with ALIMCO/district authorities.

**RBSK and the 2026 child-service transition.** The [3 May 2026 release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2257617&lang=2&reg=48) describes strengthened community-to-facility pathways, digital health cards and referral follow-up. The [24 July 2026 parliamentary reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2288846&lang=1&reg=48) establishes 38 conditions; the older NHM programme-page list of 32 is superseded for catalogue descriptions. The established 2013 framework includes free treatment and tertiary surgical follow-up for programme conditions and used Anganwadi/government/government-aided school touchpoints. Exact local 2.0 implementation, eligible-school reach and service availability still require district verification; the full 2026 PDF could not be retrieved in this research pass. [Established framework](https://nhm.gov.in/images/pdf/programmes/RBSK/Operational_Guidelines/Operational%20Guidelines_RBSK.pdf)

Separately, **SSBSK launched 29 June 2026**, combining HBNC and HBYC into a birth-to-36-month home/community framework. Its release envisages integration with the RBSK 2.0 portal. It does **not** say RBSK was abolished or its age range replaced. Use district-adopted SSBSK schedules when building infant/young-child visit rules; treat promised platform integration as a rollout claim, not a verified public API. [MoHFW launch, 29 Jun 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2279075&lang=1&reg=48)

**RKSK access design.** NHM describes four Saathiyas per village/1,000 population/ASHA habitation; ASHA facilitates selection, coordinates peer activity and helps engage families. Quarterly Adolescent Health Days and monthly clubs provide service-linkage touchpoints. AFHCs require trained clinicians/counsellors and privacy/confidentiality. Store verified clinic location, adolescent service hours and supported languages; do not require a sensitive reason for referral to be visible to an NGO dispatcher. These product choices are proposed local-validation hypotheses. [Peer programme](https://www.nhm.gov.in/index1.php?lang=1&level=3&lid=493&sublinkid=1249), [AFHCs](https://www.nhm.gov.in/index1.php?lang=1&level=3&lid=421&sublinkid=1247)

**NMHP scope and availability.** Tele-MANAS is described by DGHS as a digital NMHP component providing universal remote access and assured service linkages; it should not inflate scheme counts. “767 districts sanctioned” does not prove staffing or appointment availability in all 767. Use district confirmation to distinguish sanctioned, operational and accepting-referrals states. A local NGO directory should record professional qualifications, cost and capacity rather than classifying every volunteer as a mental-health provider. [DGHS NMHP](https://www.dghs.mohfw.gov.in/national-mental-health-programme.php), [1 Apr 2026 reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2247567&lang=1&reg=1)

**NTEP, nutrition DBT and digital access.** ₹1,000 replaces the older ₹500 nutrition figure. The DGHS NTEP overview still displays ₹500 and historic 2023 statistics, so it is suitable for programme structure/ASHA recognition, not the current transfer amount. The [9 Dec 2025 reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2200795&lang=1&reg=6) and live [MyScheme entry](https://www.myscheme.gov.in/hi/schemes/nikshay), checked 8 October 2026, support ₹1,000 for notified TB patients. The MyScheme entry describes bank-account support; the optimizer can track documentation/referral status with authorized staff. An NGO must not independently determine TB diagnosis, medication or preventive-treatment eligibility.

**Ni-kshay Mitra evidence and limits.** The 2026 guidance explicitly documents food-basket coordination/logistics delays, inconsistent support quality and digital onboarding/matching gaps. It broadens household nutrition and psychosocial support and specifies district-supervised, non-clinical volunteer activity. These are documented programme implementation challenges; the document does not establish their prevalence in every district. Its 2026 proposals for digital matching, delivery tracking and monitoring are not proof of an open third-party integration interface. [CTD guidance, 2026, programme-challenges and volunteer sections](https://tbcindia.mohfw.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Guidance-document-on-Ni-kshay-Mitra-Initiative.2026_compressed.pdf)

**Research implications to test locally.** These are hypotheses, not established failure claims: (a) document preparation plus appointment/camp routing may improve assistive-device uptake; (b) explicit referral acknowledgement may reduce lost child/adolescent follow-up; (c) matching NGOs by coverage, skills and capacity may improve transport and support completion; (d) separating NPY payment state from Ni-kshay Mitra delivery state may prevent false “benefit delivered” flags. Measure completed referrals, elapsed time, failed hand-offs and reasons, confirmed support delivery, ASHA time saved and household consent withdrawals. Preserve clinician/district authority for clinical and entitlement decisions; use authorized, consent-based data access and locally verified service directories.
