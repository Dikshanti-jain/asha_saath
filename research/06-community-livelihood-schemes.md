# Eight community, livelihood and public-service programmes

Research cutoff: **8 October 2026, India**. Exactly eight reserved programmes are counted below. These eight, the eight maternal/child programmes and the eight welfare programmes form the requested **24 additional programmes**, not 24 mutually exclusive beneficiary populations. A household may use several of them. Primary government sources establish programme design; they do not establish service availability at a particular village, facility or NGO.

**Evidence labels:** **E** = a role or mechanism expressly supported by the cited government material. **H** = a proposed local NGO complement or ASHA referral workflow to validate with the responsible department; it is not a nationally mandated task, proven local gap, or automatic funding entitlement.

**Caveats that affect the ASHA visit optimiser and local NGO pilot**

- **Separate service ownership from the visit route.** Record the responsible facility/department, referral destination, verified opening hours, service availability, required documents and last verification date. Route optimisation cannot itself guarantee a medicine, diagnostic slot, water connection, ration-card approval or loan. Proposed NGO referral assistance in this document is a local design hypothesis, not evidence of a nationwide programme failure.
- **Do not add overlapping benefits/populations.** AAM includes medicines and diagnostics supported by the two NHM initiatives; the same household can also receive NFSA foodgrains and Anganwadi services. NFSA's statutory nutrition provisions overlap with Anganwadi delivery. Count the eight programmes once each, then deduplicate people/households and service events in impact metrics. ONORC and the components subsumed in POSHAN 2.0 do not add extra rows.
- **Funding follows a different decision path.** Household service entitlement, an SHG bank loan, an NGO implementation contract, and a licensed pharmacy's operator incentive are different. The programme list does not identify an unrestricted funding source for a local NGO. Validate actual state empanelment, procurement, grant or operator rules before assigning funds or describing a funded partnership.
- **The latest POSHAN evidence reveals an existing scheduler.** The official **14 April 2026** backgrounder reports an **AWW Home Visit Scheduler integrated into Poshan Tracker in April 2026**, with **23 structured visits** for pregnant women/children up to three, automatic scheduling and counselling/video prompts. This is material prior art for a visit-optimiser proposal, while AWW and ASHA workflows remain distinct. Do not claim that frontline-worker home-visit scheduling does not exist. [PIB current POSHAN backgrounder](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2251769).
- **Use newer policy for current numbers.** The NHM programme page contains **31 August 2018** diagnostic rollout counts and old illustrative test lists; those are not October 2026 status. The cited **1 August 2025** reply gives 14 sub-centre/63 PHC tests. Likewise, the POSHAN baseline approval period ends in 2025–26, but the 2026–27 Budget and April 2026 government description show ongoing programme activity; do not infer that it ended. [NHM baseline page](https://nhm.gov.in/index1.php?lang=1&level=2&sublinkid=1218&lid=192), [2025 reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2151239), [2026–27 Budget](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/eb/vol1.pdf).
- **JJM evidence limitation:** the [new JJM 2.0 English guidelines](https://jaljeevanmission.gov.in/sites/default/files/guideline/Operational-Guidelines-JJM-2.pdf) were identified in the official index but could not be fully extracted because the PDF was too large for the browsing tool and the alternate extractor failed. Its NGO/ASHA clauses are therefore **unverified** here. Historical NGO ISA provisions are in the [December 2019 operational guidelines](https://jaljeevanmission.gov.in/sites/default/files/guideline/JJM_Operational_Guidelines.pdf), section 5.7 and institutional responsibilities; a [December 2021 official Samvad publication](https://jaljeevanmission.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-05/JalJeevanSamvad-December-2021-en.pdf) gives a state example of grassroots ASHA/NGO water-quality training. A historical state example is not a current nationwide mandate.
- **Use the correct NFSA grievance channel.** The central portal's [online grievance utility](https://nfsa.gov.in/public/frmRegisterPublicGrievance.aspx) says submitted input **does not constitute a complaint**. A pilot should verify the state's formal complaint route/District Grievance Redressal Officer rather than promise legal redress through that feedback form. [NFSA responsibilities](https://www.nfsa.gov.in/portal/NFSA-Act).

All linked primary sources were checked or identified by **8 October 2026**. Undated pages are labelled as such; page-update dates are not treated as dates on which every underlying rule changed.
