# Service breakpoints: patient, caregiver and operations review

Research cutoff: **8 October 2026, India**. Contribution 8 of the requested ten research perspectives. Read alongside `01-asha-ground-truth.md`, `04-welfare-access-schemes.md`, `03-digital-health-platforms.md` and `11-solution-design.md`. This review adds a narrow primary-evidence check and an operating model; it does not establish local partner participation, capacity, government approval or clinical effectiveness. No outreach occurred.

**Strongest practical correction:** make a named, resourced coordinator and a next action mandatory before a support case enters the provider queue. The ASHA should capture a consented barrier once, while that coordinator obtains acceptance, resolves non-response, checks fulfillment and returns a short outcome. A directory and an algorithm cannot perform those jobs. Pilot the process on paper/phone with real service offers before building a large network.

The ledger is deliberately small. Existing Ni-kshay community-support linkage, frontline task/case applications and referral platforms including Unite Us and findhelp already exist. Do not pitch “there is no referral platform.” The narrower hypothesis is that a particular district lacks a usable connection between its approved visit workflow and **fresh, eligible, reachable nonclinical service capacity with accountable follow-through**. Platform presence, referral functionality, deployment, active use and service fulfillment are different facts; the companion platform catalogue supplies the product comparison.

## Eleven handoffs: where the network may break

All fixes in the last column are **H**, proposed operating/product choices. Each needs a receiving human and a real channel, including assisted phone/paper intake when agreed.

| Handoff | Evidence status and possible break | Existing owner or required local decision | Practical fix and beneficiary protection |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Assigned household → today's visit | **E S2:** duplicate systems can impair timely records. **H:** imported due list, household availability or coordinates are stale. | ASHA confirms assigned household; ANM/supervisor approves due rules and exceptions. | Reuse approved IDs/records; show source and last update; let worker correct availability/location. Keep unlocated/remote households visible with age of deferral. No out-of-catchment reassignment by an algorithm. |
| 2. Observation → clinical escalation | **P, local 01:** ASHA facilitates; trained clinical team owns clinical decisions. **H:** a support request diverts attention from urgent assessment. | ANM/CHO/MO and established referral facility. | Clinical route always available; record escalation separately from social support. NGO response timers never determine emergency care or permission to refer. Use district-approved rules, not generated diagnoses. |
| 3. Need → public entitlement/service | **P, local 04:** government programmes have their own eligibility/agency pathways. **H:** an NGO substitutes for an accessible public service. | Relevant facility, TB unit, AWW/ICDS, social-welfare office or implementing agency; coordinator follows access barrier. | Identify service and responsible public channel first; distinguish potentially eligible, applied, sanctioned and received. Track denial/delay/document barrier. Temporary charity must preserve the unresolved public-service issue. |
| 4. Request → beneficiary agreement | **P S1:** ethical engagement requires consent/confidentiality. **H:** family or worker assumes consent to sharing. | Beneficiary; authorized caregiver participation where appropriate; trained worker records permitted decision. | Explain recipient, data and service in the preferred language. Support refusal, withdrawal and safe contact preferences. Do not make clinical visits/public benefits conditional on NGO consent. No forced patient app or OTP. |
| 5. Registered organisation → operational service branch | **H:** legal listing/office distance is mistaken for service readiness. | Participating NGO branch verifies offer; coordinator checks it with district-approved partner process. | Store branch, service, covered ward/block/hamlets, eligibility, fees, access needs, hours, contact and verified date. Organisation registration never establishes a funded local slot. Retain manual review for uncertainty. |
| 6. Eligible match → accepted capacity | **E S1:** linkage delays/coordination problems occur within an existing support programme. **H:** analogous multi-service local offers become stale. | Receiving NGO intake staff accept/decline; coordinator owns non-response and alternatives. | Explicit acceptance with expiry; no identifiable broadcast. Record full, outside catchment, ineligible, waitlisted and no response distinctly. Reserve one agreed allocation transactionally; release on cancellation. |
| 7. Acceptance → feasible appointment/journey | **E S3:** travel, waits and wage loss were access barriers. **H:** a confirmed slot still cannot be reached. | Facility/provider confirms appointment; coordinator arranges permitted logistics with recipient. | Confirm date, accessibility, documents, accompaniment, cost and return journey before travel. Combine appropriate tasks only with clinical/provider agreement. An NGO ride does not certify the destination can deliver care. |
| 8. Funded promise → supplies and last-mile delivery | **E S1:** food-basket logistics/support quality gaps are acknowledged. **P S1:** DTO/district-supervised arrangements already apply to TB support. | For TB use DTO/TU pathway; elsewhere name contracting NGO operations lead and approved coordinator. | Verify inventory/delivery responsibility and catchment; log short/failed delivery and replacement action. An approved request is not disbursement; a donor pledge is not inventory. Do not invent a general-purpose platform wallet. |
| 9. Provider says delivered → recipient confirms | **P S1:** delivery validation is prescribed. **H:** mistaken delivery or unsafe verification is counted as success. | Provider reports; recipient/authorized worker confirms; coordinator handles disputes. | Separate attempted, provider-reported, confirmed and disputed. Offer assisted confirmation without smartphone. Use proportionate private evidence; do not post patient photos, home locations or diagnoses. Production evidence rules must fit the programme rather than silently removing official requirements. |
| 10. Facility discharge → local follow-up | **E S2:** reviewed pathways show downstream continuity gaps. **H:** NGO case closure hides outstanding health follow-up. | Discharging facility supplies plan; authorized ANM/CHO/MO maps follow-up; ASHA performs assigned tasks. | Preserve separate clinical and support states. | 11. Unresolved case → supervision, grievance and network repair | **E S5:** digital aids did not automatically improve supervision. **H:** dashboard exists but no one acts. | Named coordinator, backup and supervisor; district authority resolves programme issues. | Queue with next action, due date, age and reason; human review of overdue cases and recipient complaints. Escalation windows are agreed per service. Close unresolved honestly; report empty capacity and refusals without punishing worker or beneficiary. |

## Ten-lens review matrix

Every feature/tradeoff/validation below is **H**, a design proposal. Frustrations are interview prompts supported by the indicated evidence where available, not quotations or universal claims.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ASHA** | “One more app, register and chasing task” (S2/S4); a remote family keeps slipping. | One short barrier card reusing permitted visit fields; worker-set availability; coordinator follow-up; unserved list. | NGO capture can displace essential visits; automatic metrics may penalize terrain or refusal. | Observe complete working days before/after; count extra entry, calls and escort minutes, missed due visits and unpaid work. Remove any parallel fields the district does not need. |
| **Beneficiary / caregiver** | “Referral means another trip, lost wages, repeated story or unsafe disclosure” (S3). | Recipient-chosen contact/time; plain-language offer with costs/accessibility; paper/assisted status; refusal/withdrawal. | An accessible slot may be farther away; reminders on shared phones can expose a private issue. | Journey interview including declined/unreachable cases; measure journeys, waiting, expenditure and recipient-reported completion. ## Three service cases to validate

These are synthetic operating scenarios, not real beneficiaries, secured partners or promised benefits. No clinical thresholds, drug instructions or helpline numbers are included.

### A. Rural pregnancy follow-up with a transport barrier

The health team has already marked a household due for follow-up. The optimizer includes the remote hamlet despite extra travel. At the visit, the person asks for help reaching the approved facility. ASHA records the barrier and permitted contact once; ANM/CHO handles any clinical concern through the existing route. Coordinator first checks the applicable public transport/service entitlement and actual booking channel, including JSSK where eligible; it is not a universal entitlement for every journey. If unavailable, the public-service failure stays open while an opted-in NGO is asked about a bridge ride.

The NGO office may be near the PHC but serve a different block, so it fails the catchment filter. Another offer needs an accessible vehicle, confirmed facility slot and return plan; its last confirmed capacity has expired, so coordinator reconfirms before promising the ride. If the person declines accompaniment, record the choice and offer permitted alternatives. **Validate:** completed facility attendance, public/NGO transport separately, actual household cost, essential visit coverage and ASHA/coordinator minutes. No NGO confirmation closes the medical follow-up.

### B. Rural child referred for assessment/rehabilitation

An existing RBSK/clinical pathway identifies a child needing assessment. The caregiver can make one long journey but cannot afford repeated weekly travel or missed wages. The official service/DEIC confirms current eligibility, staffing, appointments and any onward pathway; the app must not copy an old condition list or fabricate a local rehabilitation provider. Coordinator checks public treatment, rehabilitation/device channels and authorized agency requirements before arranging NGO logistics.

A nearby “disability NGO” offers document navigation but no qualified therapy; it can accept that distinct request, not substitute for clinical assessment. For ADIP/RVY or camps, verify current authorization, appropriate assessment, local dates and funded device availability; portal presence is insufficient. A provider without home service or sustainable repeat capacity is not a complete solution. Caregiver has no smartphone: worker/coordinator provides an assisted status slip and private agreed follow-up. **Validate:** assessment attended, onward service initiated and sustained where required, journeys/wage loss, reasons for discontinuation, support refusal and extra coordination time. S3 supports investigating these barriers, not predicting this family's outcome.

### C. Urban migrant household already under the TB programme

A consenting household in a vulnerable ward reports interrupted food support and has moved within the city. Care and support continue through the authorized NTEP/TB unit and Ni-kshay pathway; a new generic app does not duplicate TB registration. With permitted data, coordinator verifies current ward/contact, DTO/TU assignment, Ni-kshay Mitra linkage and actual basket delivery. Nutrition-payment state and food-support state remain separate. For other household needs, existing ration/ICDS/social-welfare channels are checked before supplementary NGO help.

S1 already supplies district-supervised responsibilities; reuse them. A local Mitra/NGO confirms whether the new ward is covered and whether the upcoming delivery has stock. An old donation pledge is insufficient. A public case map, diagnosis-bearing SMS or photograph publicity is unacceptable; offer a recipient-chosen discreet delivery/contact method. If support is declined or consent withdrawn, stop the voluntary support contact as agreed while preserving lawful programme care. **Validate:** authorized transfer/linkage acknowledged, confirmed delivery/quality, unresolved rounds, recipient privacy experience and coordinator effort. Do not interpret a delivered basket as treatment completion.

## Minimal operating agreement and pilot decision

Before opening intake, the district/implementation partner and participating providers should agree: **who coordinates and funds the work; which households/catchments are included; who approves clinical/entitlement decisions; which services are actually accepting cases; update/response arrangements; minimal data/consent; backup and grievance route; and permissible completion evidence**. This is an H pilot recommendation, not a newly asserted statutory requirement. For TB, align with the established DTO/TU structure rather than substituting a private coordinator for programme authority.

A service record should include `branch`, `service`, `covered_localities`, `eligibility_version`, `cost`, `accessibility`, `intake_hours`, `contact_channel`, `capacity_quantity`, `capacity_confirmed_at`, `valid_until`, `accepting_intake`, `responsible_person`. A need needs `consent_scope`, `safe_contact`, `case_owner`, `backup`, `next_action_at`, `public_pathway_status` and separate support/clinical status. “Unknown” is an explicit state. Match by these service facts before distance; a nation-level registration total never supplies local capacity.

Start with one willing primary-care team and a few locally confirmed offers; the exact size is a proposal to fit local coordination resources. First trace recent successful, failed and refused cases across all eleven handoffs. Then run the planner/support workflow for a defined cohort and compare with the existing process. **Burden neutrality is an acceptance criterion to test**, not a claim: no net increase in ASHA administrative/chasing time and no reduction in essential due coverage. Coordinator/provider effort is measured separately rather than hidden as free labour.

Keep denominators honest: eligible due visits, actually attempted/completed visits, unique consented support cases, offers, accepted slots, reported delivery, recipient-confirmed delivery and unresolved/withdrawn cases. Report time to resolution and reasons for failure, including no capacity, inaccessible travel, public entitlement delay and refusal. Include remote/migrant/no-smartphone households and disclose missing confirmation rather than excluding them. Measure recipient cost, privacy incidents and complaints. These operational results cannot establish causal health benefits without a suitable evaluation.

The best first demonstration is a **failed match that is visibly recovered by its owner**, while a remote due visit stays protected. If a paper/phone pilot cannot identify real capacity and an accountable receiving person, a wider digital directory should wait.
