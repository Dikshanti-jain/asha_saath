# Six additional comparators: close analogues and current policy overlap

Research date: 8 October 2026. These six entries extend the three eight-platform modules to **30 platforms/services**. They are not six unique new markets. Government tools, configurable infrastructure and overseas providers have different availability. This is public-documentation research, not authenticated testing.

| Platform / operator / geography | Documented features or announced scope | Relevance and evidence boundary | Practical implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **25. Khushi Baby CHIP / Nirogi Rajasthan**; Khushi Baby; India, with Rajasthan and other government partnerships | Integrated community/primary-health coordination, worker applications, health-official analytics and implementation support. The older annual report explicitly separates existing features from planned enhancements. [Operator overview](https://www.khushibaby.org/index.html), [2021–22 annual report](https://www.khushibaby.org/KhushiBaby_2021_22_Annual_Report.pdf) | Very relevant local comparator: an NGO already works with government on ASHA/ANM digital infrastructure. Undated newer and older webpages show different claimed reach totals; neither is used as independently verified current adoption. Their broad primary-care coordination scope should not be dismissed as a simple directory. | Ask what the pilot district already uses and explore a compatible support layer. Reviewed sources do not establish an all-NGO service-capacity network, but that absence of documentation is not proof that CHIP cannot support or already customize referrals. |
| **26. OpenSRP**; Ona and contributors; configurable international health-system software | FHIR-native Android, administration and analytics; patient/household registries, care plans, due/overdue tasks, offline sync, reporting and commodity management. [Operator documentation, features as of release v1.0.1 / Sep 2023](https://docs.opensrp.io/features/app-features), [overview](https://docs.opensrp.io/) | Existing configurable software. The current docs describe household needs, interventions and referral history/outcomes. [Immunization package](https://docs.opensrp.io/features/supported-health-domains/immunization). | **27. Public materials also describe community organizations receiving referrals and reporting services. [Community organization offering](https://uniteus.com/industries/community). Its reported outcomes and US funding/privacy ecosystem cannot be copied as evidence for an Indian district. | Study accountable partner onboarding and outcome definitions. Indian ASHA roles, programme eligibility, weak connectivity, district operations and NGO catchments still require local implementation. Do not claim closed-loop social referral as a world-first. |
| **28. Findhelp**; Findhelp; primarily United States social-care ecosystem | Configurable social-needs screening, programme recommendations, private cross-organization referrals and individual/population outcomes; integration, benefits enrollment and fulfillment are advertised in its offering. [Official platform page](https://company.findhelp.com/products/platform/) | Direct social-support comparator. Its help documentation distinguishes referral mechanisms and programme-established next steps; referrals may use existing intake channels. [Sending programme referrals](https://go.findhelp.com/findhelp/sending-program-referrals). No operational India ASHA network or India-government integration was verified. | Design assisted/local intake and explicit service-specific closure rather than pretending one workflow fits every provider. Discovery and fulfillment exist internationally; local verified capacity and governance remain execution questions. |

[Official backgrounder, 14 Apr 2026, home-visit section](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2251769) | A live, documented scheduling overlap for **AWWs**, not evidence that the same tool is every ASHA's schedule. ## Market conclusion

Public sources show a **developed category**, not an empty market. Near-term value depends on which local handoff remains unresolved after existing systems are used. The defensible pitch is a testable, small operational improvement: a feasible worker day plus a consented, service-specific request to an available local provider with an accountable outcome. Build-versus-configure and partnership decisions require real district interviews.

No US platform's marketing completion rate, total reach or financial return is used as a benchmark promise for this Indian proposal. No current government API access, NGO participation or platform integration is claimed.
