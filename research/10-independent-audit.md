# Independent audit: research credibility and build readiness

Audit date: **8 October 2026, India**. This is the tenth assigned research review. I read the decision brief, method note, research contributions 01–09, solution design 11, additional comparators 12 and the report assembler. I independently counted the catalogue rows, checked cross-file consistency and reopened five material primary sources. No application was executed, authenticated platform tested, local partner contacted, legal certification performed or patient data accessed.

**Verdict: suitable as a qualified decision report and for designing a synthetic prototype; not evidence of a ready production service.** The five consistency findings raised during this audit were corrected and the updated text was reread. The strongest direction is the required visit planner plus one narrowly scoped, consented document-navigation referral. A live pilot depends on approved rules, willing providers, current local offers and funded coordination. Those conditions have not been secured.

The audit is independent in review responsibility, not an independent replication of every source in the book. Ten distinct assignments add perspectives; they do not turn shared government webpages or operator descriptions into ten independent confirmations. Agent completion, catalogue size and source count do not certify correctness or adoption.

## Prioritised corrections and evidence gaps

1. **Resolved in the report; verify in the build:** geographic grouping is explicit, ANMOL chronology is dated, projections preserve true deadlines, import identity is stable and demo scope is consistent. The deadline/identity corrections affect overdue visibility and duplicate visits; the proposed acceptance tests must demonstrate them before any implementation is called reliable.
2. **Keep the agreed hackathon scope:** one document-navigation service, a small synthetic caseload, basic geography, feasible time accounting and a visible unresolved case. Keep the 24-programme/30-platform breadth as research, with no obligation to implement every pathway.
3. **Before a real pilot:** obtain approved current rules, worker mappings and urgent clinical routes; onboard genuinely willing providers; verify actual service catchments and capacity; fund a named coordinator; agree safe assisted consent, safeguarding, data handling, complaints and completion evidence. If there is no accountable receiving person or physical capacity, do not promise service fulfillment.
4. **Before market or impact claims:** interview local workers, beneficiaries/caregivers and intake staff; trace recent successful, refused and failed cases; compare against the existing phone/register/app process. Evaluate build-versus-configure with the systems already used locally. No current field research establishes willingness to adopt, burden neutrality, procurement, prices, demand conversion or causal clinical benefit.
5. **Before promising specific entitlements or funding:** resolve programme continuation gaps and applicable State rules, verify local service readiness, and obtain provider/funder-specific authorisation. Operator documentation and government entitlement design are different from a funded slot today.

The concise decision brief is the right entry point for mentors. The longer report is a reference book; preserve the reading-order guide, clickable sources and visibly labelled limitations. A four-minute story should explain the actual planner, one failed match recovered by its coordinator, independently confirmed support and an unresolved public-service step. Ambition is credible when the working scope and evidence boundary are clear.
