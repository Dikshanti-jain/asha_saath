# Delivery cue card and claim review

Revised 9 October 2026. This is a speaking aid, not another script to memorise.

## The six beats

| Time | Remember | Look or point |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | A list of visits; limited day; one family needs document help | Judges; no screen clicking |
| 0:40 | December 2025: over 10.40 lakh ASHAs. Two connected jobs | Judges |
| 1:10 | Priority/due date before distance; unserved reason visible | Saved plan, route order and one reason |
| 2:05 | Same family → service area/capacity → permission → coordinator | Help-request detail |
| 3:25 | ₹500 example: reserve → report → person's amount; fictional demo scope | Cash stages or a saved confirmation |
| 4:10 | Benefits → small staffed pilot → measured results → ask | Judges |

## What changed and why

- Replaced the generic “three questions / accountable workflow / innovation” framing with one continuous, explicitly illustrative story.
- Kept one national number in speech. The NFHS-6 statistic is verified but its detailed denominator distracts from the operational problem and does not prove a scheduling failure. It stays in the evidence/Q&A notes.
- Moved the test count out of the spoken emotional argument. Tests prove software behaviour; a live demonstration and an honest pilot plan make that distinction easier to understand.
- Replaced the closing slogan with the concrete purpose: someone remains responsible after the person is referred.
- Removed statements that sound like real NGO onboarding already happened. Today's examples are fictional; verification and partnership agreements are pilot work.
- Added one concrete travel-support example to explain the working cash ledger without reciting finance features. Its ₹500 amount is illustrative; no payment or actual grant is claimed.

## Make it sound like you

Speak to one mentor at a time. Pause after “help delivered bolne se request close nahi hoti.” Explain the screen with your own words. Use “hamne banaya hai” for the working prototype and “pilot mein check karenge” for expected benefits.

Do not claim “humne ASHAs se baat karke dekha” unless your team actually conducted those interviews. Keep the story introduced with “sochiye.” Do not manufacture emotional family details or add “lives saved.”

Rehearse once with a timer and once without reading. If over five minutes, shorten the scale paragraph and the list of stakeholder benefits; keep the scheduling rule, consent, confirmation and honest demo scope.

**If interrupted:** stop the script and answer the actual question. Resume from the next beat, not from the line you missed.

**Natural 20-second answer:** “Hum ASHA ka din plan karte hain. Visit mein help chahiye toh coordinator suitable NGO se connect karta hai, permission lekar. Document help ho ya demo cash support, NGO ka report kaafi nahi: person ki confirmation ke baad hi request close hoti hai.”
